# Arrival at Quba

chapter: hch_quba
stage: quba
prefix: sp_quba_chapter_

**Question:** How did the Muslims of Madinah at last meet the Prophet ﷺ at Quba, and what did he found there?

## Overview

Word had reached Madinah that the Prophet ﷺ had left Makkah.[[02:p1–p2 | ‘Urwah ibn al-Zubayr, through Ibn Shihab]] Every morning after the dawn prayer, the Muslims there walked out to the edge of the lava plain and watched for him until the sun took the last of the shade and drove them home. Most of them had never seen him.[[03:p1–p2 | Ibn Ishaq, from men of ‘Abd al-Rahman ibn ‘Uwaymir's people among the Companions]] When he came at last, they had already given up for the day, and the first to see him was not one of them at all.[[01:p1 | ‘Urwah ibn al-Zubayr]] What followed was a stay at Quba, visitors who had come a long way to reach him, and the first mosque where he prayed openly with his companions.[[sp_departure_chapter_arrival:p1, H-23833-516-822 | Ibn Ishaq]][[H-9862-84-ISHAQ-EARLY-SALMAN-04 | Salman al-Farisi]][[12:p1 | quoting Ibn ‘Abbas through Ibn ‘A'idh, al-Hakam ibn ‘Utaybah on ‘Ammar, and Jabir]]

## Every morning at the lava plain

When the Muslims in Madinah heard that the Prophet ﷺ had left Makkah, they began to go out every morning to the lava plain, the harrah, to wait for him.[[02:p1–p2 | ‘Urwah ibn al-Zubayr, through Ibn Shihab]][[01:p1 | ‘Urwah ibn al-Zubayr]] "When we had prayed the dawn prayer, we would go out to the edge of our lava plain," one of them remembered, "and by Allah, we would not leave until the sun had beaten us out of the shade." Those were hot days.[[03:p1–p2 | Ibn Ishaq, from men of ‘Abd al-Rahman ibn ‘Uwaymir's people among the Companions]]

On the road the Prophet ﷺ had met al-Zubayr, coming home from Syria with a party of Muslim merchants, and al-Zubayr had dressed him and Abu Bakr in white.[[02:p1–p2 | ‘Urwah ibn al-Zubayr, through Ibn Shihab]] Their guide brought them down through the valley of Ri'm and on to Quba, in the upper part of Madinah, where the clan of Banu ‘Amr ibn ‘Awf lived.[[H-23833-515-CONT | Ibn Ishaq]][[10:p1–p2 | Anas ibn Malik]]

One day, after a long wait, they went back to their houses. It was a Monday in Rabi‘ al-Awwal; the morning heat had grown fierce and the sun was nearly at its height.[[01:p1 | ‘Urwah ibn al-Zubayr]][[H-23833-515-CONT | Ibn Ishaq]] A Jewish man had climbed one of the town's forts to look at something of his own, and he saw them: the Prophet ﷺ and his companions, dressed in white, shimmering in the mirage. He could not hold back. "People of the Arabs!" he shouted at the top of his voice. "Here is your good fortune, the one you have been waiting for!"[[01:p1 | ‘Urwah ibn al-Zubayr]][[03:p1–p2 | Ibn Ishaq, from men of ‘Abd al-Rahman ibn ‘Uwaymir's people among the Companions]]

## Under the palm tree

The Muslims snatched up their weapons and met the Prophet ﷺ out on the lava plain. He turned with them to the right and stopped among Banu ‘Amr ibn ‘Awf.[[01:p1 | ‘Urwah ibn al-Zubayr]]

They found him sitting in the shade of a palm tree with Abu Bakr, a man of about his own age. Most of them had never seen the Prophet ﷺ before.[[03:p1–p2 | Ibn Ishaq, from men of ‘Abd al-Rahman ibn ‘Uwaymir's people among the Companions]] Abu Bakr stood to receive the people while the Prophet ﷺ sat in silence, and the Ansar, the Muslims of Madinah, crowded round and greeted Abu Bakr, unable to tell which of the two he was.[[01:p1 | ‘Urwah ibn al-Zubayr]][[03:p1–p2 | Ibn Ishaq, from men of ‘Abd al-Rahman ibn ‘Uwaymir's people among the Companions]] Then the shade moved off the Prophet ﷺ, and the sun fell on him. Abu Bakr came and held his cloak over him, and at that they knew him.[[01:p1 | ‘Urwah ibn al-Zubayr]][[03:p1–p2 | Ibn Ishaq, from men of ‘Abd al-Rahman ibn ‘Uwaymir's people among the Companions]]

## Days at Quba

The Prophet ﷺ stayed with Kulthum ibn Hidm of Banu ‘Amr ibn ‘Awf. When they came down at his house, Kulthum called out to a freedman of his, "Najih!", a name that means "successful". "Najahta," the Prophet ﷺ said: "You have succeeded."[[H-1673-4088-HS-QUBA-HOSTS-1 | on Kulthum and Sa‘d]] When he went out from Kulthum's house, he would sit with people at the house of Sa‘d ibn Khaythamah, a man without family whose home lodged the unmarried emigrants: "the house of the bachelors". So some said he stayed with Sa‘d.[[04:p1 | Ibn Ishaq]] Abu Bakr stayed with Khubayb ibn Isaf at al-Sunh; others said with Kharijah ibn Zayd.[[05:p1 | Ibn Ishaq]]

‘Ali, after three days in Makkah returning the trusts people had left with the Prophet ﷺ, set out after him and joined him at Kulthum's house.[[sp_departure_chapter_arrival:p1, H-23833-516-822 | Ibn Ishaq]] His own stay at Quba, ‘Ali said, was only a night or two, with a Muslim woman who had no husband. In the middle of the night he saw a man knock at her door and hand her something. Uneasy, ‘Ali asked who he was. "That is Sahl ibn Hunayf," she said. "He knows I am a woman with no one. When evening comes he goes to his people's idols and breaks them, then brings them to me and says, 'Use this for firewood.'" Years later, when Sahl died with him in Iraq, ‘Ali would tell this story of him.[[H-9783-1007-ALI-A, H-9783-1008-ALI-B | Ibn Ishaq, from ‘Ali ibn Hind ibn Sa‘d ibn Sahl ibn Hunayf, from ‘Ali]]

Salman, a Persian then held as a slave by a man of Banu Qurayzah, had known Madinah the moment he saw it, from his teacher's description of the land of the coming prophet. He was up one of his master's palm trees at work when his master's cousin came with the news: "They are gathered at Quba around a man who has come from Makkah, and they claim he is a prophet." Salman shook so hard he thought he would fall on his master. He climbed down asking what the news was, and his master punched him hard. "What is it to you? Get back to your work."[[H-9862-84-ISHAQ-EARLY-SALMAN-04 | Salman al-Farisi]][[H-9862-81-ISHAQ-EARLY-SALMAN-01 | the chain of Salman's story]] That evening he brought the Prophet ﷺ some food. "I had this set aside as charity," he said, "and I think you and your companions, strangers here, more deserving of it than anyone." The Prophet ﷺ held back his hand. "Eat," he told his companions, and did not eat himself. That was one of the signs: the teacher who had sent Salman to this land had told him this prophet would not eat charity.[[H-9862-84-ISHAQ-EARLY-SALMAN-04 | Salman al-Farisi]]

## The mosque at Quba

At Quba the Prophet ﷺ founded his mosque and prayed in it.[[06:p1 | Ibn Ishaq]][[01:p1 | ‘Urwah ibn al-Zubayr]] In one report ‘Ammar ibn Yasir saw the need: "The Messenger of Allah ﷺ must have a place where he can take shade when he wakes, and pray," and he gathered stones and built it. In another, the Prophet ﷺ made the place where he had stopped a place of prayer, and Banu ‘Amr ibn ‘Awf built it. The Muslims of Madinah had kept up mosques and prayer for years before he came; but this was the first mosque where the Prophet ﷺ prayed openly with his companions.[[12:p1 | quoting Ibn ‘Abbas through Ibn ‘A'idh, al-Hakam ibn ‘Utaybah on ‘Ammar, and Jabir]]

Most scholars hold that it is the mosque the Quran describes as "founded on God-consciousness from the first day", with the words "In it are men who love to purify themselves". Those words, the Prophet ﷺ said in a report from Abu Hurayrah, were revealed about the people of Quba.[[12:p2–p3, 12:p10–p13_prophet | Quran 9:108]]

## Riding on

How long he stayed, the reports differ. Ibn Ishaq counts the days from Monday to Thursday, though Banu ‘Amr ibn ‘Awf themselves said he stayed with them longer; ‘Urwah speaks of ten nights and more, and Anas of fourteen.[[06:p1 | Ibn Ishaq]][[07:p1 | Ibn Ishaq]][[01:p1 | ‘Urwah ibn al-Zubayr]][[10:p1–p2 | Anas ibn Malik]]

On a Friday, the Prophet ﷺ left them.[[07:p1 | Ibn Ishaq]] He sent for the leading men of Banu al-Najjar, and they came with their swords at their sides. Anas remembered the sight: "It is as if I can see the Messenger of Allah ﷺ on his mount, with Abu Bakr riding behind him, and the leading men of Banu al-Najjar around him."[[10:p1–p2 | Anas ibn Malik]] So the Prophet ﷺ rode out of Quba, towards Madinah.

## In depth

### How long was the stay at Quba?

The reports give different lengths. Ibn Ishaq names the days Monday to Thursday and the departure on Friday, adding that Banu ‘Amr ibn ‘Awf claimed he stayed longer; Ibn Hajar quotes him as saying five days.[[06:p1 | Ibn Ishaq]][[07:p1 | Ibn Ishaq]][[09:p1 | on the length of the stay]] ‘Urwah's report in al-Bukhari says "ten nights and more", and Anas says fourteen nights.[[01:p1 | ‘Urwah ibn al-Zubayr]][[10:p1–p2 | Anas ibn Malik]] Ibn Hajar lists still others: three days in Musa ibn ‘Uqbah's report from Ibn Shihab, and twenty-two nights in a report from Mujammi‘ ibn Harithah. He prefers Anas: Anas belonged to the Khazraj, not to Banu ‘Amr ibn ‘Awf, who were of the Aws, and he stated his number plainly.[[09:p1 | on the length of the stay]] In some copies of al-Bukhari, Anas's number reads "twenty-four"; Ibn Hajar judges "fourteen", the reading of the other transmitters, correct.[[14:p1 | on the reading "twenty-four"]] He also suggests that some short counts simply leave out the day of arrival or of departure.[[08:p1 | on the date of arrival]]

### Which day did he arrive?

The reports agree on a Monday in Rabi‘ al-Awwal but not on the date. Ibn Ishaq gives the twelfth of the month;[[H-23833-515-CONT | Ibn Ishaq]] Ibn Hajar also cites the first, the second, the seventh, the thirteenth and the middle of the month, and explains some of the difference by when the new moon was sighted. Most reports have him arriving by day, one by night; Ibn Hajar reconciles them: he arrived at the end of the night and entered by day.[[08:p1 | on the date of arrival]]

### Kulthum or Sa‘d?

Ibn Ishaq gives both hosts and does not decide between them: "Allah knows best which it was; we have heard both." Those who name Kulthum explain that the Prophet ﷺ sat with people at Sa‘d's house, the house of the bachelors.[[04:p1 | Ibn Ishaq]] Al-Hakim judged Kulthum the more likely, saying Ibn Shihab knew this best; Ibn Hajar quotes reports supporting each, and the same way of joining them: he lodged with Kulthum and sat with his companions at Sa‘d's.[[H-1673-4088-HS-QUBA-HOSTS-1 | on Kulthum and Sa‘d]] Abu Bakr's host is likewise given two ways, Khubayb ibn Isaf or Kharijah ibn Zayd.[[05:p1 | Ibn Ishaq]]

### Who tells these scenes?

The watch on the lava plain, the Jewish man's shout and Abu Bakr's cloak come from two reports that agree closely: ‘Urwah ibn al-Zubayr's, in al-Bukhari, and one that Ibn Ishaq relates from ‘Abd al-Rahman ibn ‘Uwaymir ibn Sa‘idah, who had it from men of his own people among the Prophet's ﷺ Companions; the quoted memory of the shade is theirs.[[01:p1 | ‘Urwah ibn al-Zubayr]][[03:p1–p2 | Ibn Ishaq, from men of ‘Abd al-Rahman ibn ‘Uwaymir's people among the Companions]][[03:p1 | Ibn Ishaq's chain]] ‘Ali's story of Sahl ibn Hunayf comes through Ibn Ishaq, who heard it from a descendant of Sahl; al-Tabari preserves it, and adds that ‘Ali used to tell it after Sahl died with him in Iraq.[[H-9783-1007-ALI-A, H-9783-1008-ALI-B | Ibn Ishaq, from ‘Ali ibn Hind ibn Sa‘d ibn Sahl ibn Hunayf, from ‘Ali]] Salman told his own story to Ibn ‘Abbas, from whom Ibn Ishaq has it.[[H-9862-84-ISHAQ-EARLY-SALMAN-04 | Salman al-Farisi]][[H-9862-81-ISHAQ-EARLY-SALMAN-01 | the chain of Salman's story]]

### The mosque founded on God-consciousness

The words about ‘Ammar come from al-Hakam ibn ‘Utaybah, in Yunus ibn Bukayr's additions to Ibn Ishaq's work, and the words of Ibn ‘Abbas from Ibn ‘A'idh; Ibn Hajar quotes both. He also cites Jabir: the Muslims of Madinah had kept up mosques and prayer for years before the Prophet ﷺ came. That is why he calls Quba the first mosque built for the Muslims as a whole, where the Prophet ﷺ prayed with them openly.[[12:p1 | quoting Ibn ‘Abbas through Ibn ‘A'idh, al-Hakam ibn ‘Utaybah on ‘Ammar, and Jabir]]

Scholars differ over which mosque the verse means. Most say Quba, the plain sense of the verse. Yet Abu Sa‘id reports that when two men disputed the question, the Prophet ﷺ answered, "It is this one", meaning his own mosque in Madinah, "and in that one", meaning Quba, "there is much good." Ibn Hajar, with al-Dawudi and al-Suhayli, concludes that both were founded on God-consciousness, and someone he quotes reads "from the first day" as pointing to Quba, founded on the first day the Prophet ﷺ came to the land of his migration.[[12:p2–p3, 12:p10–p13_prophet | Quran 9:108]][[12:p3–p9_hajar, 12:p13–p15 | Abu Sa‘id, al-Qurtubi, al-Dawudi, al-Suhayli]]

## For the reviewer

- **Decisions to confirm.** (1) The chapter ends as the Prophet ﷺ rides out of Quba with Abu Bakr behind him and Banu al-Najjar around him; the Friday prayer among Banu Salim, the invitations on the way, the camel kneeling and Abu Ayyub are left to `hch_madinah_arrival` (its current account already opens the road with the Friday prayer). (2) Ibn Ishaq's "Friday" and Anas's picture of the ride out are told together as one departure; Anas does not name the day. (3) The two welcome reports (‘Urwah and Ibn Ishaq's informants) are woven into one scene, since they agree; the quotation about the shade is attributed to "one of them". (4) The length of the stay is given once, plainly, as a difference between Ibn Ishaq, ‘Urwah and Anas. (5) Kulthum is told as the host, with Sa‘d's house as where he sat with people, following the explanation Ibn Ishaq records; the other view is stated in one clause. (6) Salman's first visit is told as a Quba scene (his later visits, in Madinah, are not). Suhayb's arrival at Quba ("Abu Yahya, the trade has profited!", al-Bayhaqi 2/523, archive `H-7478-1044-RA-SUHAYB`) was drafted here and cut for length and because `hch_early_migration` tells Suhayb's escape; restore it in one sentence if the sequence read wants Quba to receive him.
- **New readings:** Ibn Ishaq on the arrival time and route (note 4); ‘Ali joining at Kulthum's house, shared with the departure chapter (note 7); ‘Ali and Sahl ibn Hunayf, from al-Tabari (note 8); Salman at Quba, from Ibn Ishaq's *al-Siyar wa al-Maghazi* (notes 9, 21); Ibn Hajar on the two hosts (note 19).
- **Gaps.** None the story needs. The archive has more Quba material not used (al-Samhudi on Kulthum's date-drying yard as the mosque site, through Ibn Zabalah; Musa ibn ‘Uqbah's report of a first stop at ‘Abdullah ibn Ubayy's; a weak report of the camel at Quba), kept out as weaker or off the line of the story.
- **Removed from the account:** the Madinah mosque material (the date-drying yard of Sahl and Suhayl, the bargaining with Banu al-Najjar, the graves and palms, the building chant: `01:p2–p4`, `10:p3–p4_cont`, `11`, `13`), which belongs to `hch_madinah_arrival` and `hch_mosque`; Ibn Hajar's lists of dates and durations, the readings of "twenty-four", and the debate over the verse (now in In depth); every line about how reports should be read.
