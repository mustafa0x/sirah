# Questions: Departure from Makkah

## hch_departure_q1
level: beginner
scope: lesson
answer: b
sources: `sp_departure_chapter_council:p1–p3`

Why did Quraysh's leaders meet in Dar al-Nadwah?

### a

To settle a quarrel between two of their clans.

> Dar al-Nadwah was where Quraysh settled their affairs, but this meeting had one subject: the Prophet ﷺ.

### b

They feared he would join his companions in Madinah, out of their reach.

> Right. His companions had found a home and protection there, and Quraysh feared those people would fight Makkah for him.

### c

To plan an attack on the Muslims already in Madinah.

> Their worry was the Prophet ﷺ still in Makkah, and what would happen if he left.

### why

Watching the Muslims leave one after another, Quraysh's leaders met to decide what to do with the Prophet ﷺ before he could follow them.

## hch_departure_q2
level: beginner
scope: lesson
answer: c
sources: `sp_departure_chapter_decision:p2–p3`

Which plan did the council agree on in the end?

### a

To chain him behind a locked door until he died.

> That was proposed first, and rejected: word would slip out, and his companions would come to free him.

### b

To drive him out of their land.

> That was rejected too: the old man warned that he would win over a tribe and come back at its head.

### c

That a young man from every clan strike him together.

> Right. This was Abu Jahl's plan: his blood would be spread across all the clans, so his own people could not fight them all.

### d

To offer him wealth and leadership to stop preaching.

> No such offer was made at this council. They chose between prison, exile and killing.

### why

Abu Jahl's plan shared the blame among every clan, so Banu ‘Abd Manaf would have to accept blood money instead of revenge.

## hch_departure_q3
level: intermediate
scope: lesson
answer: a
sources: `sp_departure_chapter_decision:p2–p3`

Why did the old man from Najd reject driving the Prophet ﷺ out of Makkah?

### a

His fine speech would win over a tribe, and he would return at its head.

> Right. "Have you not seen how fine his speech is," the old man asked, "and how he wins men's hearts?"

### b

His companions would come and free him by force.

> That was the objection to locking him up, not to driving him out.

### c

Exile would anger his clan, Banu ‘Abd Manaf.

> The clan came up only with Abu Jahl's plan, which spread his blood so the clan could not take revenge.

### why

Driving him out would only give him the chance to win followers elsewhere and come back stronger.

Who slept in the Prophet's ﷺ bed that night, and what was he wrapped in?

### a

Abu Bakr, in his own cloak.

> Abu Bakr was at his own house. The Prophet ﷺ gave his bed to someone else.

### b

‘Ali, in a borrowed cloak of Abu Bakr's.

> The cloak was the Prophet's ﷺ own, the one he himself slept in.

### c

‘Amir ibn Fuhayrah, in a black cloak.

> ‘Amir appears later, on the road. That night it was ‘Ali in the bed.

### d

‘Ali, in the Prophet's ﷺ green cloak from Hadramawt.

> Right. "Sleep on my bed, and wrap yourself in this cloak of mine," he told ‘Ali, "nothing you dislike will reach you from them."

### why

‘Ali lay in the Prophet's ﷺ bed and cloak, so the men watching the door thought he was still inside.

## hch_departure_q5
level: intermediate
scope: lesson
answer: b

As he walked out among the men at his door, what did the Prophet ﷺ do?

### a

He waited until they had fallen asleep, then slipped past.

> They were waiting for him to sleep, not the other way round. He came out to them while they were there.

### b

He scattered dust over their heads, reciting the opening of Ya-Sin.

> Right. He recited up to "So We have covered them, and they do not see," and not a man was left without dust on his head.

### c

He left by a window at the back of his house.

> He came out to them through their midst. The small back door was at Abu Bakr's house, later.

### d

He disguised himself in ‘Ali's clothes.

> It was ‘Ali who wore the Prophet's ﷺ cloak. Allah took the men's sight from him, so they did not see him.

### why

Allah took their sight from him, and he walked through their midst, scattering dust and reciting Ya-Sin.

## hch_departure_q6
level: intermediate
scope: lesson
answer: c
sources: `sp_departure_chapter_trust:p1`, `sp_departure_chapter_arrival:p1`

Why did ‘Ali stay behind in Makkah after the Prophet ﷺ left?

### a

To keep watch on Quraysh and send news of any pursuit.

> His task was not to spy. It was to give back what people had left in the Prophet's ﷺ care.

### b

To bring Abu Bakr's family to Madinah.

> Abu Bakr's family is not his task here. ‘Ali stayed to deal with the trusts.

### c

To return the valuables people had left with the Prophet ﷺ.

> Right. Many in Makkah trusted his honesty with their valuables, and ‘Ali stayed three days and nights to return every one.

### d

Because Quraysh held him back by force.

> He stayed by choice, with a task given to him, and then set out to join the Prophet ﷺ.

### why

Even as he left, the Prophet ﷺ made sure every trust was returned; only then did ‘Ali follow, joining him at the house of Kulthum ibn Hidm.

## hch_departure_q7
level: beginner
scope: lesson
answer: a
sources: `H-23833-508-807`, `E24`

Which way did the Prophet ﷺ and Abu Bakr go when they set out that night?

### a

South, towards Mount Thawr below Makkah.

> Right. They left through a small door at the back of Abu Bakr's house and headed south, away from the road to Madinah.

### b

North, straight onto the road to Madinah.

> That is the road anyone looking for them would expect. They went the other way.

### c

East, to hide among the tents at Mina.

> Mina plays no part here. They made for Mount Thawr, to the south.

### why

They left by night in the opposite direction from Madinah, to Mount Thawr on the road south towards Yemen.

## hch_departure_q8
level: expert
scope: source_study
answer: b
sources: `sp_departure_chapter_council:p1–p3`, `sp_departure_chapter_saw1:p1`, `sp_departure_chapter_saw2:p1`, `sp_departure_chapter_exit:p3`

How are the reports of the council (from Ibn ‘Abbas) and of the dust at the door (from Muhammad ibn Ka‘b) graded?

### a

The council is sound, and only the dust report is weak.

> Al-Suwayani grades the council report hasan, but others judge it weak, and that is the stronger view.

### b

Both are judged weak, though the story still tells them.

> Right. The council, the warning and ‘Ali in the bed rest on a weak report, and so do the dust and Ya-Sin.

### c

Both are sound, recorded by al-Bukhari.

> Both come through Ibn Ishaq, not al-Bukhari, and both are judged weak.

### d

The dust report is sound, since it names its narrator.

> Muhammad ibn Ka‘b was of the next generation and did not see the events himself; his report is judged weak too.

### why

Al-Suwayani accepted the council report except for the mention of Iblis, but the stronger view judges it weak, as it does the dust report.

## hch_departure_q9
level: intermediate
scope: source_study
answer: d
sources: `sp_departure_chapter_urwa:p1–p4`

‘Urwah ibn al-Zubayr links Quraysh's plot to a verse of al-Anfal. What does the verse name?

### a

Their plan to buy off his clan with blood money.

> Blood money belongs to Abu Jahl's plan in Ibn ‘Abbas's report. The verse names the plot itself.

### b

The dust thrown on the heads of the men at the door.

> The dust comes from Muhammad ibn Ka‘b's report, not ‘Urwah's.

### c

The cave where the two would hide.

> ‘Urwah mentions the cave at Thawr, but the verse speaks of what Quraysh plotted.

### d

Plots to restrain him, kill him or drive him out.

> Right. "To restrain you, or kill you, or drive you out" (al-Anfal 8:30): the same three options the council debated.

### why

‘Urwah's shorter account ties the night to al-Anfal 8:30, whose three options match the council's three proposals.

## hch_departure_q10
level: intermediate
scope: lesson
answer: c
sources: `sp_departure_chapter_hazchain:p1`, `sp_departure_chapter_haztext:p1–p2`

What did the Prophet ﷺ once say to Makkah, standing at al-Hazwara?

### a

"I will return to you at the head of an army."

> Those were Quraysh's fears at the council, not his words to Makkah.

### b

"Your people have driven me out, and I will not look back."

> His words were full of love for the city, not a farewell in anger.

### c

"You are the dearest of Allah's lands to me."

> Right. "Had I not been driven out of you," he said, "I would not have left."

### d

"I have been commanded to leave you for a better land."

> He did not call Madinah better. He called Makkah the best of Allah's lands.

### why

Makkah was his home; he left it only because he was driven out.
