# On the Coastal Road

chapter: hch_road
stage: onward

**Question:** Who rode with the Prophet ﷺ and Abu Bakr after the cave, and what happened on the road by the sea?

## Overview

After three nights hidden in the cave, the camels came at last, and with them the man who would show the way.[[sp_guide_bukhari_coast:p1 | ‘Aishah]][[H-23833-509-808 | Ibn Ishaq]] Four riders set out: two of them hunted men, one a freed slave, and one a guide who did not share their faith.[[sp_guide_sira_name:p1–p2 | Ibn Ishaq from Asma: "they were four"]][[sp_guide_bukhari_hire:p1 | ‘Aishah]] He led them down to the sea.[[H-23833-514-818 | Ibn Ishaq]] On the road, strangers who knew Abu Bakr asked him about the man riding in front of him, and one blazing noon a long rock offered shade.[[sp_guide_anas_recognition:p2 | Anas ibn Malik]][[H-1727-7453-ROAD-A, H-1727-7453-ROAD-B | al-Bara from Abu Bakr]] But the pursuit had not ended in Makkah.[[H-1681-5911-HS-ROAD-AND-AISHA-FEVER-1 | al-Bara from Abu Bakr]]

## Four riders

The guide had come with the camels, and now there were four of them: the Prophet ﷺ, Abu Bakr, Abu Bakr's freedman ‘Amir ibn Fuhayrah, and the guide.[[sp_guide_sira_name:p1–p2 | Ibn Ishaq from Asma: "they were four"]] They mounted and set off. Abu Bakr took ‘Amir up behind him on his camel, to serve them on the way,[[sp_permission_h_23833_510_cont:p1 | Ibn Ishaq]] and at times let him ride it in turn. No one else went with them.[[sp_thawr_exp_h_7478_1001_ra_urwa_musa_cave:p3 | Musa ibn ‘Uqbah's report]]

## The road by the sea

The guide led them down below Makkah, then out along the coast,[[H-23833-514-818 | Ibn Ishaq]] "by the edge of the sea," as ‘Aishah described it.[[H-7478-996-RA-AISHA-LONG | ‘Aishah, through ‘Urwah and al-Zuhri]] Along the shore they went until they came out on the road below ‘Usfan; then he took them by lower Amaj, and on through valleys and mountain passes, one after another.[[H-23833-514-818 | Ibn Ishaq]]

People knew Abu Bakr. His hair had turned grey, and he had often passed among the people of Madinah on his trading journeys; the Prophet ﷺ, whose hair had not greyed, they did not know.[[sp_guide_anas_recognition:p2 | Anas ibn Malik]][[sp_guide_hajar_age_start:p1 | Abu Bakr had greyed and was known from his trading journeys; the Prophet ﷺ had not greyed]] In fact, the Prophet ﷺ was the older of the two, by more than two years.[[sp_guide_hajar_age_end:p1, p4 | the Prophet ﷺ older than Abu Bakr by more than two years]] Men would meet them and ask, "Abu Bakr, who is this man in front of you?" "This man guides me on the way," he would answer. Whoever heard it thought he meant the road. He meant the way of good.[[sp_guide_anas_recognition:p2 | Anas ibn Malik]] The Prophet ﷺ had told Abu Bakr, "Keep people busy away from me, for a prophet should not lie," and when they asked who was with him, Abu Bakr said, "A guide who guides me."[[sp_guide_bayhaqi_start:p1–p2, sp_guide_bayhaqi_end:p1 | Abu Hurayra]]

## Shade at noon

Years later, Abu Bakr told the story of one day on that road. He had bought a camel saddle from ‘Azib for thirteen dirhams,[[H-1727-7453-ROAD-A, H-1727-7453-ROAD-B | al-Bara from Abu Bakr]][[H-7478-1004-RA-BARA-SURAQA, H-7478-1005-RA-BARA-SURAQA | al-Bara from Abu Bakr]] and ‘Azib would not let his son al-Bara carry it home until Abu Bakr told them how he and the Prophet ﷺ had fared when they went out with the idolaters hunting them.[[H-7478-1004-RA-BARA-SURAQA, H-7478-1005-RA-BARA-SURAQA | al-Bara from Abu Bakr]] "Watchers had been set for us," Abu Bakr said, "so we went out by night."[[H-1681-5911-HS-ROAD-AND-AISHA-FEVER-1 | al-Bara from Abu Bakr]]

They pressed on through the night and the next day, until the sun stood at its height[[H-1681-5911-HS-ROAD-AND-AISHA-FEVER-1 | al-Bara from Abu Bakr]] and the road lay empty, with no one passing on it.[[H-1727-7453-ROAD-A, H-1727-7453-ROAD-B | al-Bara from Abu Bakr]] Abu Bakr cast his eyes about for any shade to shelter in,[[H-7478-1004-RA-BARA-SURAQA, H-7478-1005-RA-BARA-SURAQA | al-Bara from Abu Bakr]] and a long rock rose into view, with shade the sun had not yet reached. They dismounted there. With his hand he smoothed a place in its shade where the Prophet ﷺ could sleep, spread a fur over it, and said, "Sleep, Messenger of Allah, and I will look about for you."[[H-1727-7453-ROAD-A, H-1727-7453-ROAD-B | al-Bara from Abu Bakr]] The Prophet ﷺ lay down, and Abu Bakr went out to see whether any of the pursuers were in sight.[[H-7478-1004-RA-BARA-SURAQA, H-7478-1005-RA-BARA-SURAQA | al-Bara from Abu Bakr]]

What he saw was a shepherd boy driving his sheep towards the same rock, wanting from it what they had wanted: its shade.[[H-7478-1004-RA-BARA-SURAQA, H-7478-1005-RA-BARA-SURAQA | al-Bara from Abu Bakr]] "Whose are you, boy?" Abu Bakr asked, and the boy named his master. "Is there milk in your sheep?" "Yes." "Will you milk for me?" "Yes."[[H-1681-5911-HS-ROAD-AND-AISHA-FEVER-1 | al-Bara from Abu Bakr]] The boy took hold of a ewe. Abu Bakr told him to shake the dust from its udder, then to shake the dust from his hands.[[H-7478-1004-RA-BARA-SURAQA, H-7478-1005-RA-BARA-SURAQA | al-Bara from Abu Bakr]] When al-Bara told the story, he struck one hand against the other to show how.[[H-1727-7453-ROAD-A, H-1727-7453-ROAD-B | al-Bara from Abu Bakr]] Then the boy milked a little milk into a wooden bowl.[[H-1727-7453-ROAD-A, H-1727-7453-ROAD-B | al-Bara from Abu Bakr]]

Abu Bakr carried a leather water-flask with a cloth over its mouth, kept for the Prophet ﷺ to drink from and to make his ablutions. He poured water over the milk until it was cool to the bottom.[[H-1681-5911-HS-ROAD-AND-AISHA-FEVER-1 | al-Bara from Abu Bakr]][[H-1727-7453-ROAD-A, H-1727-7453-ROAD-B | al-Bara from Abu Bakr]] He did not want to wake the Prophet ﷺ, but when he came back he found him already awake.[[H-1727-7453-ROAD-A, H-1727-7453-ROAD-B | al-Bara from Abu Bakr]] "Drink, Messenger of Allah," he said, and the Prophet ﷺ drank, "until I was content," Abu Bakr said.[[H-1681-5911-HS-ROAD-AND-AISHA-FEVER-1 | al-Bara from Abu Bakr]]

## On their trail

The Prophet ﷺ then asked, "Has the time to leave not come?" "It has," Abu Bakr said.[[H-1727-7453-ROAD-A, H-1727-7453-ROAD-B | al-Bara from Abu Bakr]] They set off once the sun had passed its height,[[H-1727-7453-ROAD-A, H-1727-7453-ROAD-B | al-Bara from Abu Bakr]] and behind them the pursuit was on their trail.[[H-1681-5911-HS-ROAD-AND-AISHA-FEVER-1 | al-Bara from Abu Bakr]] People were out searching for them, and of all of them, only one would catch up: a man on a horse.[[H-7478-1004-RA-BARA-SURAQA, H-7478-1005-RA-BARA-SURAQA | al-Bara from Abu Bakr]]

## In depth

### The guide's name

‘Aishah's report in al-Bukhari describes the guide by his clan, Banu al-Dil, and his skill, but does not name him.[[sp_guide_bukhari_hire:p1 | ‘Aishah]] The name comes from Ibn Ishaq, through Asma bint Abi Bakr, who counted the travellers as four: the Prophet ﷺ, Abu Bakr, ‘Amir ibn Fuhayrah and "‘Abdullah ibn Arqat, their guide". Ibn Hisham adds, "it is also said: ‘Abdullah ibn Urayqit," the form used in this app.[[sp_guide_sira_name:p1–p2 | Ibn Ishaq from Asma: "they were four"]] The modern commentator Hani Faqih notes the same: the Sahih has "a man of Banu al-Dil", and Ibn Ishaq gives the name.[[sp_guide_hani_name:p1–p3]]

### A guide who did not share their faith

The guide followed the religion of Quraysh, yet the two trusted him with their camels and with the secret of their route.[[sp_guide_bukhari_hire:p1 | ‘Aishah]] Al-Qurtubi quotes ‘Aishah's report and two scholars' conclusions from it: al-Muhallab, that people of another faith may be trusted with secrets and property when they are known to be faithful and honourable; and Ibn al-Mundhir, that Muslims may hire them as guides.[[sp_guide_qurtubi:p1–p3 | ‘Aishah's report quoted from al-Bukhari; al-Muhallab and Ibn al-Mundhir]]

### The road, place by place

Ibn Ishaq names the guide's route as a chain of places: below Makkah, along the coast, out onto the road below ‘Usfan, by lower Amaj, back to the road after Qudayd, then al-Kharrar, Thaniyyat al-Marrah, Liqf, and on through a series of valleys and passes to al-‘Arj, and from there to Batn Ri'm and Quba. Ibn Hisham records other spellings for several of the names, and adds that at al-‘Arj one of their mounts lagged, and a man of Aslam, Aws ibn Hujr, carried the Prophet ﷺ to Madinah on his camel.[[H-23833-514-818 | Ibn Ishaq]][[H-23833-515-CONT | the route from al-‘Arj to Batn Ri'm and Quba]] Musa ibn ‘Uqbah, in al-Bayhaqi, describes the opening of the route the same way: below Makkah, the coast below ‘Usfan, and past Qudayd.[[sp_thawr_exp_h_7478_1001_ra_urwa_musa_cave:p3 | Musa ibn ‘Uqbah's report]]

### One day, three tellings

Abu Bakr's account of the noon rest reaches us through al-Bara ibn ‘Azib, in al-Bukhari, in Muslim and in al-Bayhaqi, with small differences. Asked whose he was, the shepherd answers "so-and-so" in al-Bukhari, "a man of Madinah" in Muslim, and "a man of Quraysh", whom Abu Bakr recognised, in al-Bayhaqi.[[H-1681-5911-HS-ROAD-AND-AISHA-FEVER-1 | al-Bara from Abu Bakr]][[H-1727-7453-ROAD-A, H-1727-7453-ROAD-B | al-Bara from Abu Bakr]][[H-7478-1004-RA-BARA-SURAQA, H-7478-1005-RA-BARA-SURAQA | al-Bara from Abu Bakr]] In Muslim it is the Prophet ﷺ who asks, "Has the time to leave not come?"; in al-Bayhaqi, Abu Bakr says, "The time to leave has come."[[H-1727-7453-ROAD-A, H-1727-7453-ROAD-B | al-Bara from Abu Bakr]][[H-7478-1004-RA-BARA-SURAQA, H-7478-1005-RA-BARA-SURAQA | al-Bara from Abu Bakr]] Abu Bakr tells the journey from the night they left Makkah and does not mention the cave, so the reports do not say on which day of the road the noon rest fell. In all three, the pursuit follows right after it; in Muslim and al-Bayhaqi, the rider who catches up is Suraqah ibn Malik.[[H-1727-7453-ROAD-A, H-1727-7453-ROAD-B | al-Bara from Abu Bakr]][[H-7478-1004-RA-BARA-SURAQA, H-7478-1005-RA-BARA-SURAQA | al-Bara from Abu Bakr]]

### "An older man", "a young man"

Anas describes Abu Bakr as a *shaykh*, an older man, and the Prophet ﷺ as a *shabb*, a young man. Ibn Hajar explains that Abu Bakr's hair had turned grey and the Prophet's ﷺ had not, and that people knew Abu Bakr from his trading journeys. In fact, he adds, the Prophet ﷺ was the older of the two, by more than two years.[[sp_guide_hajar_age_start:p1 | Abu Bakr had greyed and was known from his trading journeys; the Prophet ﷺ had not greyed]][[sp_guide_hajar_age_end:p1, p4 | the Prophet ﷺ older than Abu Bakr by more than two years]]

### "A guide who guides me"

Al-Bayhaqi records a similar report from Abu Hurayra: the Prophet ﷺ told Abu Bakr, "Keep people busy away from me, for a prophet should not lie." When Abu Bakr was asked, "What are you?", he answered, "A seeker." When asked who was with him, he said, "A guide who guides me."[[sp_guide_bayhaqi_start:p1–p2, sp_guide_bayhaqi_end:p1 | Abu Hurayra]] Both this report and Anas's set these meetings on the way into Madinah.[[sp_guide_anas_recognition:p2 | Anas ibn Malik]][[sp_guide_bayhaqi_start:p1–p2, sp_guide_bayhaqi_end:p1 | Abu Hurayra]]

## For the reviewer

- **Decisions to confirm.** (1) The chapter opens with the guide's arrival as a one-sentence hand-over from Thawr, and refers back to the hiring in one sentence (told in `hch_preparations`); the Prophet's ﷺ purchase of the camel "at its price", which Ibn Ishaq places at the cave, stays in preparations. (2) The name is given as ‘Abdullah ibn Urayqit (Ibn Hisham's alternative) per the brief; Ibn Ishaq's own form, Ibn Arqat, is explained in In depth. (3) Abu Bakr's answer "This man guides me on the way" is told in the road section as a recurring scene. Both Anas and Abu Hurayra set it on the way into Madinah. If the reviewer wants strict chronology, move it to `hch_quba`. In Anas's report a horseman catches up right after, so placing it before Suraqah fits. (4) The noon rest is told as "one day on that road" without saying which day: Abu Bakr's account starts from Makkah by night and skips the cave (explained in In depth). (5) The account ends on "only one would catch up: a man on a horse", leaving Suraqah's name, the reward and the chase to `hch_suraqa`. (6) The ages "older man" / "young man" in Anas's wording are softened in the account to "his hair had turned grey… whose hair had not greyed", following Ibn Hajar, with the literal wording explained in In depth.
- **New readings:** the guide's arrival with a third camel and ‘Amir riding behind (Ibn Hisham 1/486–487); Musa ibn ‘Uqbah on riding in turn (al-Bayhaqi 2/480); Ibn Ishaq's route (Ibn Hisham 1/491–492); ‘Aishah's "*yad bahr*" (al-Bayhaqi 2/475); the noon rest and the shepherd in al-Bukhari 3917, Muslim 2009 and al-Bayhaqi 2/483–484. Notes 5 and 6 cite Core packets from the preparations and Thawr topics; the rest are archive records with no packet yet and need packets in the build.
- **Gaps:** none of the sources gives the length of the journey in days or the distances between the stations, or where on the road the noon rest fell, so the account says none of this. The meeting with al-Zubayr and his white clothes (al-Bukhari 3906) is left to `hch_quba`, where it opens the Madinah-waiting passage in the same hadith. Ibn Hisham's al-‘Arj episode (Aws ibn Hujr) appears only in In depth; the sequence plan should decide whether a later chapter tells it.
- **Removed from the account:** the long discussion of who named the guide and why the Sahih does not, the jurists' conclusions about hiring him, and Asma's report of the voice and verses heard in Makkah (its verses name Umm Ma‘bad's tent, which would give away a later chapter). The first two are now in In depth.
