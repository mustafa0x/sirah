# Permission and Preparations

chapter: hch_preparations
stage: preparations
prefix: sp_

**Question:** How did the long wait for permission end, and how were the Prophet ﷺ and Abu Bakr made ready to leave?

## Overview

After the pledges at al-‘Aqabah, the Muslims of Makkah set out for Madinah in small groups, to brothers and a home where they would be safe. The Prophet ﷺ stayed behind, waiting for Allah's permission to follow them.[[permission_h_23833_491_prep_778:p1 | Ibn Ishaq; the Prophet ﷺ quoted]] Abu Bakr was ready to go too, but the Prophet ﷺ asked him to wait, hinting that he might not travel alone. So Abu Bakr waited, and while he waited he fed two riding camels.[[permission_h_1681_5899_wait:p1 | ‘Aishah, through al-Zuhri from ‘Urwah]][[permission_h_23833_503_800:p1, departure_chapter_wait:p1–p4 | Ibn Ishaq]] Then one day, at an hour when no one expected a visitor, the Prophet ﷺ came to his door.[[permission_h_1681_5899_visit:p1 | ‘Aishah, through al-Zuhri from ‘Urwah]]

## Waiting in Makkah

When the Prophet ﷺ sent the Muslims of Makkah ahead, he had told them what the place was like: "I have been shown the land you will emigrate to, a land of palm trees between two lava fields."[[permission_h_1681_5899_wait:p1 | ‘Aishah, through al-Zuhri from ‘Urwah]] They left in small groups,[[permission_h_23833_491_prep_778:p1 | Ibn Ishaq; the Prophet ﷺ quoted]] and most of those who had once emigrated to Abyssinia came back and went on to Madinah as well.[[permission_h_1681_5899_wait:p1 | ‘Aishah, through al-Zuhri from ‘Urwah]]

The Prophet ﷺ stayed behind in Makkah, waiting for his Lord to give him permission to leave.[[permission_h_23833_491_prep_778:p1 | Ibn Ishaq; the Prophet ﷺ quoted]] Before long, of the emigrants only ‘Ali ibn Abi Talib and Abu Bakr were left with him, apart from those who were held back by force or had been turned from their faith.[[permission_h_23833_503_800:p1, departure_chapter_wait:p1–p4 | Ibn Ishaq]]

Abu Bakr asked him again and again for permission to emigrate. Each time the answer was the same: "Do not hurry. Perhaps Allah will give you a companion." And each time Abu Bakr hoped that the companion would be the Prophet ﷺ himself.[[permission_h_23833_503_800:p1, departure_chapter_wait:p1–p4 | Ibn Ishaq]] Once he had made himself ready to go, and the Prophet ﷺ stopped him: "Take your time, for I hope to be given permission." "Do you really hope for that, may my father be your ransom?" Abu Bakr asked. "Yes."[[permission_h_1681_5899_wait:p1 | ‘Aishah, through al-Zuhri from ‘Urwah]]

So Abu Bakr held himself back, to travel with him. He was a man of means, and he bought two riding camels and kept them at his house.[[permission_h_23833_507_804:p1 | Ibn Ishaq]] For four months he fed them on the leaves of the samur, a desert acacia, to have them ready for the day they would be needed.[[permission_h_1681_5899_wait:p1 | ‘Aishah, through al-Zuhri from ‘Urwah]][[permission_h_23833_507_804:p1 | Ibn Ishaq]]

## A visit at noon

Every day, without fail, the Prophet ﷺ came to Abu Bakr's house at one end of the day, in the morning or in the evening.[[permission_h_23833_507_805:p1–p2 | ‘Aishah, through Ibn Ishaq from "someone I do not suspect" from ‘Urwah]] One day ‘Aishah and her family were sitting in Abu Bakr's house in the full heat of noon when someone said to Abu Bakr, "Here is the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, with his head covered, at an hour when he never comes to us." "May my father and mother be his ransom," said Abu Bakr. "By Allah, only something important brings him at this hour."[[permission_h_23833_507_805:p1–p2 | ‘Aishah, through Ibn Ishaq from "someone I do not suspect" from ‘Urwah]][[permission_h_1681_5899_visit:p1 | ‘Aishah, through al-Zuhri from ‘Urwah]]

The Prophet ﷺ asked to come in, and was let in. Abu Bakr moved back from his couch so that he could sit. Only Abu Bakr's two daughters were with him, ‘Aishah and Asma.[[permission_h_23833_507_805:p1–p2 | ‘Aishah, through Ibn Ishaq from "someone I do not suspect" from ‘Urwah]][[permission_h_23833_508_cont:p1 | ‘Aishah, through Ibn Ishaq; the hiring of the guide]] "Send out those who are with you," the Prophet ﷺ said. "They are only my two daughters," Abu Bakr answered. "What is it, may my father and mother be your ransom?" "Allah has given me permission to leave, and to emigrate."[[permission_h_1681_5899_visit:p1 | ‘Aishah, through al-Zuhri from ‘Urwah]][[permission_h_23833_508_cont:p1 | ‘Aishah, through Ibn Ishaq; the hiring of the guide]]

## Companionship

"Companionship, Messenger of Allah?" Abu Bakr asked. "Companionship," he answered.[[permission_h_23833_508_cont:p1 | ‘Aishah, through Ibn Ishaq; the hiring of the guide]] ‘Aishah never forgot what she saw next. "By Allah, before that day I never knew that anyone could weep for joy," she said, "until I saw Abu Bakr weeping that day."[[permission_h_23833_508_cont:p1 | ‘Aishah, through Ibn Ishaq; the hiring of the guide]]

Then Abu Bakr turned to the camels he had waited with for so long. "Prophet of Allah, these are two riding camels I have kept ready for this." "Take one of them," he said. "At its price," the Prophet ﷺ answered.[[permission_h_1681_5899_visit:p1 | ‘Aishah, through al-Zuhri from ‘Urwah]][[permission_h_23833_508_cont:p1 | ‘Aishah, through Ibn Ishaq; the hiring of the guide]]

## A guide for the road

The two needed someone who knew the desert roads well. They hired ‘Abdullah ibn Urayqit, of the tribe of Banu al-Dil, a *khirrit*, a guide skilled at finding the way. He was a sworn ally of the family of al-‘As ibn Wa'il of Quraysh, and he still followed the religion of Quraysh.[[permission_h_23833_508_cont:p1 | ‘Aishah, through Ibn Ishaq; the hiring of the guide]][[guide_bukhari_hire:p1, guide_bukhari_coast:p1 | ‘Aishah, through al-Zuhri from ‘Urwah]] Yet they trusted him. They handed their two camels over to him, and he kept and grazed them until the time they had set. They agreed where and when he would bring the camels back: at the cave of Thawr, after three nights, on the morning of the third.[[permission_h_23833_508_cont:p1 | ‘Aishah, through Ibn Ishaq; the hiring of the guide]][[guide_bukhari_hire:p1, guide_bukhari_coast:p1 | ‘Aishah, through al-Zuhri from ‘Urwah]] From there he would lead them by the coast road.[[guide_bukhari_hire:p1, guide_bukhari_coast:p1 | ‘Aishah, through al-Zuhri from ‘Urwah]]

## Asma's waistband

At home, the family worked as quickly as they could. "We got them ready with all speed," ‘Aishah said, "and made them food for the journey in a bag."[[provisions_aisha:p1 | ‘Aishah]] Asma prepared the provisions in her father's house. But when it came to tying up the bag of food and the waterskin, there was nothing to tie them with.[[provisions_asma_house:p1 | Asma bint Abi Bakr]]

"By Allah, I can find nothing to tie them with except my waistband," Asma told her father. It was the band of cloth she wore around her waist. "Split it in two," Abu Bakr said. "Tie the waterskin with one piece and the food with the other." She did, and from that day she was known as Dhat al-Nitaqayn, "she of the two waistbands."[[provisions_asma_house:p1 | Asma bint Abi Bakr]]

The camels were with the guide, a companion had been granted, and the provisions were tied and ready. After months of hoping, Abu Bakr's waiting was over; the Prophet ﷺ had his permission to go.[[permission_h_1681_5899_visit:p1 | ‘Aishah, through al-Zuhri from ‘Urwah]][[permission_h_23833_508_cont:p1 | ‘Aishah, through Ibn Ishaq; the hiring of the guide]][[provisions_asma_house:p1 | Asma bint Abi Bakr]]

## In depth

### Two tellings of the noon visit

The visit comes to us from ‘Aishah through ‘Urwah ibn al-Zubayr, in two lines of transmission. In al-Zuhri's, recorded by al-Bukhari, the Prophet ﷺ comes with his head covered, Abu Bakr answers "they are your family", the permission is "I have been given permission to leave", and Abu Bakr's offer of a camel ends with "At its price."[[permission_h_1681_5899_visit:p1 | ‘Aishah, through al-Zuhri from ‘Urwah]] In Ibn Ishaq's, which he heard from "someone I do not suspect" from ‘Urwah, ‘Aishah adds that the Prophet ﷺ normally came morning or evening, that she and Asma were the only ones present, and that Abu Bakr wept for joy; it has "my two daughters" and "Allah has given me permission to leave, and to emigrate."[[permission_h_23833_507_805:p1–p2 | ‘Aishah, through Ibn Ishaq from "someone I do not suspect" from ‘Urwah]][[permission_h_23833_508_cont:p1 | ‘Aishah, through Ibn Ishaq; the hiring of the guide]] The story above draws on both, and gives each line where it is the only one to have it.

### "At its price"

In al-Bukhari, the Prophet ﷺ answers Abu Bakr's offer with two words: "At its price."[[permission_h_1681_5899_visit:p1 | ‘Aishah, through al-Zuhri from ‘Urwah]] Ibn Ishaq tells the exchange more fully, and later, at the moment Abu Bakr brought the camels to him and offered him the better one: "I do not ride a camel that is not mine." "Then it is yours." "No. What did you pay for it?" "Such and such." "I take it at that price."[[permission_h_23833_509_810:p1, permission_h_23833_510_cont:p1 | Ibn Ishaq]] Neither report gives the amount.

### One waistband or two?

The reports describe Asma's waistband differently. In ‘Aishah's words, Asma cut a piece of her waistband and tied the mouth of the food bag with it, and was called "she of the waistband".[[provisions_aisha:p1 | ‘Aishah]] In Asma's own words, she split it in two, for the waterskin and the food, and was called "she of the two waistbands".[[provisions_asma_house:p1 | Asma bint Abi Bakr]] Al-Waqidi, recorded by Ibn Sa‘d, joins the two: one piece tied the bag and the other became a strap for the waterskin's mouth.[[thawr_exp_h_146_241_support_and_guide:p2–p3 | al-Waqidi]] Ibn Ishaq places the waistband later, when the travellers were setting out from the cave: Asma had brought the food but forgotten a strap, so she untied her waistband and hung the food with it. Ibn Hisham adds that scholars he heard called her "she of the two waistbands", because she hung the food with one half and wore the other.[[provisions_ishaq_cave:p1, provisions_hisham_names:p1–p3 | Ibn Ishaq; then Ibn Hisham]] This telling follows Asma's own account, which places the waistband at the packing in her father's house.

### The guide's name

Al-Bukhari's report does not name the guide; it calls him a man of Banu al-Dil and explains *khirrit* as "one skilled at guiding".[[guide_bukhari_hire:p1, guide_bukhari_coast:p1 | ‘Aishah, through al-Zuhri from ‘Urwah]] Ibn Ishaq names him ‘Abdullah ibn Arqat and says his mother was of Banu Sahm; Ibn Hisham notes that he is also called ‘Abdullah ibn Urayqit, the form al-Waqidi uses.[[permission_h_23833_508_cont:p1 | ‘Aishah, through Ibn Ishaq; the hiring of the guide]][[guide_sira_name:p1–p2 | Ibn Ishaq from Asma, naming "‘Abdullah ibn Arqat, their guide"]][[thawr_exp_h_146_241_support_and_guide:p2–p3 | al-Waqidi]]

### Four months

The four months in ‘Aishah's report are the time Abu Bakr spent feeding the camels while he waited, not the length of the journey.[[permission_h_1681_5899_wait:p1 | ‘Aishah, through al-Zuhri from ‘Urwah]]

## For the reviewer

- **Decisions to confirm.** (1) The noon visit is told as one scene from both lines of ‘Aishah's report (head covered and "At its price" from al-Bukhari; the daily habit, the couch, the two daughters and the tears from Ibn Ishaq); In depth sets out which detail comes from which. (2) Asma's waistband is told at the packing, following her own account in al-Bukhari; Ibn Ishaq's placing of it at the cave, and Ibn Hisham's explanation, are in In depth only. (3) Ibn Ishaq's full price dialogue ("I do not ride a camel that is not mine") happens when the camels are brought to the cave, so the account gives only "At its price" and In depth gives the rest. (4) The guide is called ‘Abdullah ibn Urayqit, the form most of the app uses; Ibn Ishaq's text has ibn Arqat, explained in In depth. (5) The hiring of the guide, including the appointment at Thawr after three nights, is told here; the Thawr and road chapters should refer back to it in a sentence rather than retell it. (6) "a desert acacia" glosses *samur*; it is a term explanation, not a sourced fact.
- **New readings:** the companions sent ahead (note 1), waiting in Makkah with "Do not hurry" (note 3; the same passage the departure draft uses as `wait`), hiring the guide in al-Bukhari (note 8), the guide's name (note 14), and al-Waqidi's waistband and guide (note 13).
- **Gaps:** the day and month of the noon visit; the amount paid for the camel ("such and such"); how long passed between the visit and the night of departure.
- **Removed from the account:** Asma and her grandfather Abu Quhafa with the stones, and Abu Bakr taking all his money (these happen after the departure; the departure draft already tells the money, note 15 there), ‘Amir ibn Fuhayrah riding behind Abu Bakr (the road), the critic's correction of Bodley (no longer needed), the source-by-source comparison of pronouns and titles (reduced to one In depth section), and the chains of narrators.
- **Hand-overs.** Opens after the pledges at al-‘Aqabah, with the emigrants leaving and the Prophet ﷺ waiting, which picks up the end of `hch_aqaba` and refers back to `hch_early_migration` in a sentence. Ends with everything ready and the waiting over, without the night itself, so `hch_departure` can open on Quraysh's council "while the Prophet ﷺ waited in Makkah for permission". Note for the sequence read: departure's opening clause says he was still waiting; if the council is placed after the noon visit, that clause may need adjusting there.
