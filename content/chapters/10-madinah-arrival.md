# Arrival in Madinah

chapter: hch_madinah_arrival
stage: arrival

**Question:** How did Madinah welcome the Prophet ﷺ, and how was the place where he would stay chosen?

## Overview

After his days at Quba, the Prophet ﷺ rode on into Madinah, with Abu Bakr behind him and men of Banu al-Najjar around him, swords at their sides.[^1] The whole town came out to see him. People crowded the streets and climbed onto the rooftops, asking one another which of the riders he was.[^8][^9] Clan after clan stepped into his path to offer him a home, with their numbers and strength to protect him.[^4] To every one of them he gave the same answer, and let his camel walk on.[^5]

## Out of Quba, on a Friday

It was a Friday when he rode out of Quba.[^2] On the way, the time for the Friday prayer came while he was among Banu Salim ibn ‘Awf, and he prayed it in their mosque in the bed of the valley of Ranuna. It was the first Friday prayer he prayed in Madinah.[^3]

## "Let her go her way"

Then ‘Itban ibn Malik and ‘Abbas ibn ‘Ubadah came to him with men of Banu Salim. "Messenger of Allah," they said, "stay with us. We have the numbers, the arms and the strength to protect you." He answered, speaking of his camel: "Let her go her way; she is under orders." So they let her go, and she walked on.[^4]

At the homes of Banu Bayadah, Ziyad ibn Labid and Farwah ibn ‘Amr met him with their men: "Messenger of Allah, come to us, to our numbers, our arms and our strength." At Banu Sa‘idah it was Sa‘d ibn ‘Ubadah and al-Mundhir ibn ‘Amr; at Banu al-Harith ibn al-Khazraj, Sa‘d ibn al-Rabi‘, Kharijah ibn Zayd and ‘Abdullah ibn Rawahah. Each time came the same offer and the same answer, and each time they stood aside.[^5] In ‘Urwah's telling, men even began to pull at her reins.[^6]

Then she passed the homes of Banu ‘Adi ibn al-Najjar, his own kin on his mother's side: Salma bint ‘Amr, the mother of his grandfather ‘Abd al-Muttalib, had been one of their women. "Messenger of Allah," said Salit ibn Qays and Abu Salit, "come to your mother's people." "Let her go her way," he said again. "She is under orders." And she walked on.[^7]

## "Which one is he?"

The town had been waiting a long time. Anas remembered running with the other boys whenever someone cried, "Muhammad has come!", and finding nothing there, again and again.[^8] Now he had really come. The cry went round the town, "The Prophet of Allah has come! The Prophet of Allah has come!", and people climbed up high to look.[^9] Anas remembered the Ansar, the Muslims of Madinah, coming out to meet the two travellers, some five hundred of them, with the words "Go on, safe and obeyed," and the Prophet ﷺ and Abu Bakr riding on in their midst. The people of Madinah poured out, and young women on the rooftops strained to see him: "Which one is he? Which one is he?"[^8]

"I never saw the people of Madinah rejoice over anything as they rejoiced over the Messenger of Allah ﷺ," said al-Bara' ibn ‘Azib, "until even the slave girls were saying, 'The Messenger of Allah has come!'"[^11] Years later, Anas set that day beside only one other: "I saw the day he came to us and the day he died, and I have seen no two days like them."[^8]

## Where she knelt

At last the camel came to the homes of Banu Malik ibn al-Najjar and knelt, at the spot where the door of his mosque would later stand. At that time it was a yard where dates were spread out to dry,[^13][^14] and some of the Muslims used to pray there.[^14]

The Prophet ﷺ did not dismount. She sprang up and went a little way, while he left her rein slack and did not turn her with it. Then she looked behind her, went back to the place where she had first knelt, and knelt there again. She shifted, settled, and laid her neck along the ground.[^13] "This, if Allah wills, is the stopping place," he said, in ‘Urwah's report.[^14]

He dismounted, and Abu Ayyub Khalid ibn Zayd picked up his saddle and carried it into his house, and the Prophet ﷺ became his guest.[^15] When a man asked him where he would stay, he answered, "A man goes with his saddle, wherever it is."[^17]

## Under Abu Ayyub's roof

The house had two floors. The Prophet ﷺ took the lower one, and Abu Ayyub and Umm Ayyub lived above him. Abu Ayyub could not bear it. "Prophet of Allah, may my father and mother be your ransom," he said, "I hate to be above you while you are below me. Go up and stay on the upper floor, and we will come down." "Abu Ayyub," he answered, "it is easier for us, and for those who come to see us, to be on the lower floor."[^18]

So they stayed above him. One day a jar of water broke upstairs. Abu Ayyub and Umm Ayyub soaked it up with a wrap, the only blanket they had, afraid that a drop might fall on the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and trouble him.[^19] One night Abu Ayyub woke and said, "We are walking above the head of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ!" and they moved to one side for the rest of the night. "The lower floor is easier," the Prophet ﷺ told him again. But Abu Ayyub would not give way: "I will not go up onto a roof with you beneath it." So the Prophet ﷺ moved upstairs, and Abu Ayyub moved down.[^20]

They cooked his supper and sent it to him. When the rest came back, Abu Ayyub and Umm Ayyub looked for the place his fingers had touched and ate from there, hoping for blessing. One night they put onion or garlic in it, and it came back with no mark of his hand.[^21] Abu Ayyub went to him in alarm. "Is it forbidden?" he asked. "No," said the Prophet ﷺ, "but I dislike it because of its smell." "Then I dislike what you dislike," said Abu Ayyub.[^22] After that, they never cooked that plant for him again.[^21]

He stayed in Abu Ayyub's house until his mosque and his rooms were built.[^23] Close by, where the camel had knelt, the yard of drying dates was waiting.[^13][^16][^17]

## In depth

### Two tellings of the ride

Ibn Ishaq and ‘Urwah ibn al-Zubayr tell the arrival in two steps: a stay among Banu ‘Amr ibn ‘Awf at Quba, then a ride into the town on a Friday, with clan after clan offering him a home until the camel knelt.[^2][^4][^14] Anas, in one of his reports in al-Bukhari, gives the same two steps: fourteen nights at Quba, then the ride with Banu al-Najjar around him "until he reached Abu Ayyub's courtyard."[^1] In another report in al-Bukhari, Anas tells it without the stop at Quba. The Prophet ﷺ halted beside the lava field outside Madinah and sent for the Ansar. They greeted him and Abu Bakr, said "Ride on, safe and obeyed," and surrounded them with weapons. He rode on until he dismounted beside Abu Ayyub's house. There he asked, "Which of our people's houses is nearest?" "Mine, Prophet of Allah," said Abu Ayyub. "This is my house, and this is my door." "Then go and prepare a place for us to rest."[^9][^16] The story above follows Ibn Ishaq's order and uses Anas's memories of the welcome without fixing the moment each one belongs to. Anas's report of the five hundred Ansar, in al-Bayhaqi, also does not mention Quba.[^8] In another report there, he says he never saw a day more beautiful or full of light than the day the Prophet ﷺ entered.[^12]

### "Our people"

When the Prophet ﷺ asked which house of "our people" was nearest, he meant Banu al-Najjar. Ibn Hajar explains that they were his kin through the women of the family: the mother of his grandfather ‘Abd al-Muttalib was one of them.[^24] Ibn Ishaq names her as Salma bint ‘Amr, of Banu ‘Adi ibn al-Najjar.[^7]

### Where the camel knelt

Ibn Ishaq places the kneeling at the yard where the door of the mosque later stood, and has Abu Ayyub carry the saddle into his house.[^13][^15] ‘Urwah also places it at the site of the mosque.[^14] Anas speaks of the Prophet ﷺ reaching Abu Ayyub's courtyard, or dismounting beside his house.[^1][^16] Ibn Hajar quotes a report through al-Hakim, from Ishaq ibn Abi Talhah from Anas, in which the camel knelt at Abu Ayyub's own door.[^25] The modern hadith scholar al-Albani judges that report, in its full form, unreliable ("munkar"): one of its narrators, Ibrahim ibn Sirmah, is very weak.[^26] In Ibn al-Zubayr's report the camel first knelt elsewhere, rose when people urged him to stop, and knelt again where the pulpit of the mosque would be. Abu Ayyub then said, "My house is the nearest of houses to you," and took his saddle.[^17]

### Girls with drums, and "Tala‘a al-Badru"

Two songs are often told of this day. In the same report through al-Hakim, girls of Banu al-Najjar came out beating drums and singing, "We are girls of Banu al-Najjar: what a fine neighbour Muhammad is!"[^10] Al-Albani notes that a sounder report of girls with drums belongs to a wedding, not to the arrival.[^26] The song "Tala‘a al-badru ‘alayna" ("The full moon has risen over us, from the passes of al-Wada‘") is related by al-Bayhaqi from Ibn ‘A'ishah, one of Ahmad ibn Hanbal's teachers, who does not say who told him.[^27] Al-Bayhaqi notes that scholars tell it of this arrival from Makkah, and he also gives it at the return from Tabuk. Al-Albani judges the report weak, with three or more narrators missing from its chain, and records Ibn al-Qayyim's view that the passes of al-Wada‘ lie on the Syria side of Madinah, where no one coming from Makkah would pass.[^28] That is why the story above does not include either song.

### The two floors

Ibn Ishaq's report from Abu Ayyub, through Abu Ruhm, ends with the Prophet ﷺ still below and the family above; it tells the broken jar from there.[^18][^19] In the report Muslim records through Aflah, Abu Ayyub's freed slave, the family moved to one side one night, Abu Ayyub refused to stay above him, and the Prophet ﷺ moved upstairs.[^20] Ibn Hajar cites the same ending from Abu Ayyub's report in al-Hakim: Abu Ayyub "kept asking" until the Prophet ﷺ moved up.[^24] The story above tells the jar while the family was still above, and the move after it.

### Onion or garlic

Ibn Ishaq's report says "onion or garlic", unsure which. In it the Prophet ﷺ gives his reason as "I am a man who speaks in private. As for you, eat it," and the family ate it. In Muslim's two reports it is garlic. Abu Ayyub asks whether it is forbidden, and the Prophet ﷺ says it is not, "but I dislike it because of its smell."[^21][^22] Muslim gives one more chain, from Yahya ibn Sa‘id from Shu‘bah, without repeating the text.[^29]

### How long at Abu Ayyub's?

Ibn Ishaq says only that he stayed until his mosque and his rooms were built.[^23] Ibn Sa‘d, as Ibn Hajar quotes him, puts the stay at seven months.[^17][^24] In Ibn al-Zubayr's report, he stayed twelve nights in a shaded shelter at the site until the mosque was built.[^17] Ibn Ishaq gives the day he left Quba as a Friday.[^2] A modern biographer, al-Mubarakfuri, reckons the date of his arrival among Banu al-Najjar as Friday, 12 Rabi‘ al-Awwal of the first year of the Hijrah (27 September 622).[^30]

## For the reviewer

- **Decisions to confirm.**
  1. **The main line is Ibn Ishaq's**: leaving Quba on a Friday, the first Friday prayer, the clans' invitations, the camel kneeling twice, Abu Ayyub taking the saddle. Anas's welcome memories (boys running, five hundred Ansar, rooftops) and Bukhari 3911's "The Prophet of Allah has come!" are woven into the welcome without being tied to a precise moment. In depth explains that Bukhari 3911 has no stop at Quba.
  2. **The kneeling** combines Ibn Ishaq (knelt, rose, looked back, knelt again) with ‘Urwah's quotation "This, if Allah wills, is the stopping place" (Bukhari 3906). The yard is named only as a yard for drying dates where some Muslims prayed. Its owners (the two orphans), their guardian, the question "Whose is the yard?" and the purchase are left to `hch_mosque`.
  3. **The floors.** The account tells the jar while the family was upstairs (Ibn Ishaq), then the night Abu Ayyub refused and the Prophet ﷺ moved up (Muslim, through Aflah, supported by al-Hakim's report as Ibn Hajar cites it). This ordering harmonises the two reports. In depth says plainly that Ibn Ishaq's report ends with the family still above.
  4. **The garlic dialogue** in the account follows Muslim ("Is it forbidden?… because of its smell… Then I dislike what you dislike"). The supper, the marks of his fingers and "never cooked that plant for him again" come from Ibn Ishaq. Ibn Ishaq's "I am a man who speaks in private. As for you, eat it" is in In depth. Please confirm "speaks in private" for «أُناجي». The usual explanation (his private conversation with Allah and the angel) is not stated in any reading here, so it is left out; add it only with a supporting reading.
  5. **The Friday prayer at Banu Salim** is told here, as the first scene on the road into Madinah. The current `hch_quba` chapter also mentions it, and its new draft should end as they set out from Quba.
  6. **The girls with drums and "Tala‘a al-Badru"** are kept out of the account. In depth explains why (al-Albani judges the drum report "munkar" and "Tala‘a al-Badru" weak). The journey's `E33` caption should be checked against this.
  7. The overview footnotes point to passages told in the account. Its phrase "the whole town came out" rests on Anas ("the people of Madinah came out").
- **New readings:** the kneeling and the saddle (Ibn Hisham 1/495–496); ‘Urwah's "stopping place" (Bukhari 3906); Anas's arrival hadith with Abu Ayyub's "this is my door" (Bukhari 3911); al-Hakim's drum report as Ibn Hajar quotes it (7/261); Ibn al-Zubayr's "A man goes with his saddle" (al-Bayhaqi 2/509) and Ibn Sa‘d via Ibn Hajar 7/246 and 7/252; al-Albani on the drum report and on "Tala‘a al-Badru"; al-Bayhaqi 2/506–507 and 5/266; al-Rahiq's date (`E39`). All of them are in `evidence.jsonl` or the journey passages.
