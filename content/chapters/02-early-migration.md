# The First Emigrants Leave Makkah

chapter: hch_early_migration
stage: setting
prefix: sp_earlier_exp_

**Question:** How did the Prophet's ﷺ companions make their way from Makkah to Madinah ahead of him?

## Overview

In Makkah the believers were being insulted and harmed, and now the Prophet ﷺ told them they could go: Allah had given them brothers, and a home where they would be safe.[[h_146_237_permission:p1–p2 | al-Waqidi, from al-Zuhri and from ‘Urwah from ‘Aishah]][[h_9783_994_early_migrants:p1 | the Prophet ﷺ orders his companions to emigrate]] But leaving was not simple. A family could be pulled apart before it was out of town; a man could be talked back by his mother's vow; everything a man had earned could be the price of the road.[[h_23833_492_prep_cont:p1–p2 | Umm Salama, through Ibn Ishaq from his father from Salamah ibn ‘Abdullah]][[h_23833_497_prep_786:p1, h_23833_498_prep_cont:p1 | ‘Umar]][[h_23833_500_prep_791:p2–p3 | Ibn Hisham, "it was mentioned to me", from Abu ‘Uthman al-Nahdi, "it reached me"]] One household after another slipped away, and Quraysh watched their houses stand empty.[[h_23833_493_prep_781:p1, h_23833_494_prep_cont:p1–p7 | Ibn Ishaq: Banu Jahsh, Abu Ahmad, the empty house, ‘Utbah and Abu Jahl]]

## A family torn apart

Even before the great pledge at al-‘Aqabah, one man had set out. Abu Salama ibn ‘Abd al-Asad, a cousin of the Prophet ﷺ, had emigrated once before, to Abyssinia, taking his wife with him.[[h_9862_213_ishaq_early_abusalama:p2 | Yunus ibn Bukayr's transmission]] Back in Makkah, Quraysh made life hard for him, and when he heard that the believers had brothers in Madinah, he set out to join them: the first of the Prophet's ﷺ companions from Quraysh to go.[[h_23833_491_prep_780:p1, h_930_737_prep_abu_salama_date:p1 | Ibn Ishaq]]

His wife, Umm Salama, told what happened. Abu Salama set her on his camel with their little son Salama in her lap and led it out by the halter. Men of her clan, Banu al-Mughirah, blocked his way. "You have got the better of us over yourself," they said, "but why should we let you take this woman of ours about the land?" They tore the halter from his hand and took her back. Abu Salama's clan, angry, claimed the boy, and the two clans pulled him between them until they dislocated his arm. His father's people carried him off, hers kept her, and Abu Salama went on to Madinah.[[h_23833_492_prep_cont:p1–p2 | Umm Salama, through Ibn Ishaq from his father from Salamah ibn ‘Abdullah]]

"So I was parted from my husband and from my son," she said. Every morning she went out and sat at al-Abtah, weeping, until a year had passed, or nearly.[[h_23833_492_prep_cont:p1–p2 | Umm Salama, through Ibn Ishaq from his father from Salamah ibn ‘Abdullah]]

## "It is Yathrib"

Then came the night when seventy men of Madinah pledged themselves to the Prophet ﷺ at al-‘Aqabah, and he was at ease: Allah had given him protectors, a people ready for war.[[h_146_237_permission:p1–p2 | al-Waqidi, from al-Zuhri and from ‘Urwah from ‘Aishah]][[h_146_238_early_migrants:p1 | al-Waqidi]] But Quraysh knew now that the believers meant to leave, and pressed them harder than ever. The companions asked leave to emigrate, and one day the Prophet ﷺ came out to them full of joy. "I have been told where you will emigrate: it is Yathrib. Whoever wants to leave, let him go there."[[h_146_237_permission:p1–p2 | al-Waqidi, from al-Zuhri and from ‘Urwah from ‘Aishah]] "Allah has made brothers for you," he told them, "and a home where you will be safe."[[h_9783_994_early_migrants:p1 | the Prophet ﷺ orders his companions to emigrate]] They made ready, helping one another, and slipped away in secret, group after group.[[h_146_237_permission:p1–p2 | al-Waqidi, from al-Zuhri and from ‘Urwah from ‘Aishah]][[h_9783_994_early_migrants:p1 | the Prophet ﷺ orders his companions to emigrate]]

Whole households went. ‘Abdullah ibn Jahsh took his family and his brother Abu Ahmad, a blind poet who walked all over Makkah without a guide; their whole clan left with them, men and women.[[h_23833_493_prep_781:p1, h_23833_494_prep_cont:p1–p7 | Ibn Ishaq: Banu Jahsh, Abu Ahmad, the empty house, ‘Utbah and Abu Jahl]][[h_23833_495_prep_cont:p1 | Ibn Ishaq]] One day ‘Utbah ibn Rabi‘ah, al-‘Abbas ibn ‘Abd al-Muttalib and Abu Jahl passed the house of Banu Jahsh. It stood locked and deserted, its doors swinging. ‘Utbah sighed. "The house of Banu Jahsh is empty of its people," he said. "And what are you weeping over?" said Abu Jahl. "A nobody, son of a nobody." Then he said: "This is the work of this man's nephew. He has split our community, scattered our affairs, and cut the ties between us."[[h_23833_493_prep_781:p1, h_23833_494_prep_cont:p1–p7 | Ibn Ishaq: Banu Jahsh, Abu Ahmad, the empty house, ‘Utbah and Abu Jahl]]

## Held back on the road

‘Umar ibn al-Khattab planned to leave with ‘Ayyash ibn Abi Rabi‘ah and Hisham ibn al-‘As. They would meet at al-Tanadub: "Whoever of us is not there by morning has been held back, and the other two go on." In the morning only ‘Umar and ‘Ayyash were there. Hisham had been stopped and put to the test, and he gave way.[[h_23833_497_prep_785:p1–p3, h_7478_982_ra_umar_hisham:p1–p2, h_7478_983_ra_umar_hisham:p1 | ‘Umar, through Nafi‘ from Ibn ‘Umar]]

The two reached Madinah and stayed at Quba. There Abu Jahl and his brother al-Harith came after ‘Ayyash, who was their cousin and their brother through his mother. "Your mother has vowed that no comb will touch her hair, and that she will not shade herself from the sun, until she sees you." "They only want to turn you from your religion," ‘Umar warned him. "If lice troubled your mother, she would comb her hair; if the heat of Makkah grew hard on her, she would find shade." "I will keep my mother's oath," ‘Ayyash said, "and I have wealth there that I can take." When even half ‘Umar's wealth would not keep him, ‘Umar gave him his own she-camel: "If anything about them makes you uneasy, escape on her."[[h_23833_497_prep_786:p1, h_23833_498_prep_cont:p1 | ‘Umar]]

On the road Abu Jahl asked to take a turn on that camel: his own had become hard to ride. The moment they had all made their camels kneel and were on the ground, the two brothers fell on ‘Ayyash, bound him, and took him back into Makkah.[[h_23833_497_prep_786:p1, h_23833_498_prep_cont:p1 | ‘Umar]] One of ‘Ayyash's family remembered them bringing him in by daylight, tied up, calling out: "People of Makkah, deal with your fools the way we have dealt with this fool of ours."[[h_23833_498_prep_cont:p2–p3 | Ibn Ishaq from an unnamed member of ‘Ayyash's family]]

The emigrants used to say that Allah would accept no repentance from those who gave way under trial, and the two said it of themselves. Then, in Madinah, verses came down: "Say: O My servants who have wronged themselves, do not despair of the mercy of Allah." ‘Umar wrote them out by hand and sent them to Hisham, who read them at Dhu Tuwa, going up and down the valley with them, until he understood they were about men like him. He mounted his camel and rode to join the Prophet ﷺ.[[h_23833_498_prep_787:p1–p3, h_23833_499_prep_cont:p1–p4, h_7478_983_ra_umar_hisham:p1–p4 | ‘Umar, then Hisham ibn al-‘As]]

Suhayb paid a different price. As Abu ‘Uthman al-Nahdi heard it, Quraysh would not let him leave with the wealth he had made among them, so he gave it all to them in return for his freedom. When the Prophet ﷺ heard, he said: "Suhayb has profited, Suhayb has profited."[[h_23833_500_prep_791:p2–p3 | Ibn Hisham, "it was mentioned to me", from Abu ‘Uthman al-Nahdi, "it reached me"]]

## A home among the Ansar

In Makkah, a man of Umm Salama's clan at last took pity on her and told the others to let her go. Her son was given back, and she rode out alone with him in her lap. At al-Tan‘im she met ‘Uthman ibn Talhah. "Where are you going, daughter of Abu Umayyah?" "To my husband in Madinah." "Is no one with you?" "No, by God, only Allah and this little son of mine." "By God, you will not be left alone," he said, and took her camel by the halter.[[h_23833_492_prep_cont:p1–p2 | Umm Salama, through Ibn Ishaq from his father from Salamah ibn ‘Abdullah]] From stop to stop he led it, always stepping away while she mounted and dismounted, all the way to the village of Banu ‘Amr ibn ‘Awf at Quba. "Your husband is in this village," he said. "Go in, with Allah's blessing." Then he turned back to Makkah.[[h_23833_492_prep_cont:p1–p2 | Umm Salama, through Ibn Ishaq from his father from Salamah ibn ‘Abdullah]][[h_23833_493_prep_cont:p1 | Umm Salama]] "I know of no household in Islam that suffered what the family of Abu Salama suffered," she would say, "and I never saw a companion more noble than ‘Uthman ibn Talhah."[[h_23833_493_prep_cont:p2 | Umm Salama, through Ibn Ishaq]] He was not yet a Muslim; he accepted Islam at the truce of al-Hudaybiyah.[[h_23833_493_prep_foot_3:p1]]

The emigrants kept coming, and the Ansar, the "helpers" of Madinah, took them into their homes, sheltered them and shared what they had;[[h_146_238_early_migrants:p1 | al-Waqidi]] they even drew lots for who would house them.[[h_1681_5922_hs_billeting_1:p2 | Umm al-‘Ala']] Until the Prophet ﷺ came, the emigrants at Quba prayed behind Salim, the freedman of Abu Hudhayfah, because he knew more of the Qur'an than any of them.[[h_146_238_early_migrants:p1, h_7478_983_ra_salim:p1, h_7478_984_ra_salim:p1–p2, h_930_747_prep_salim:p1–p2 | Salim at Quba; Ibn ‘Umar; Ibn ‘Umar, through Ya‘qub ibn Sufyan]]

In Makkah, Quraysh raged at the young men who had slipped away.[[h_146_238_early_migrants:p1 | al-Waqidi]] In the end, of all the believers, only the Prophet ﷺ, Abu Bakr and ‘Ali were left there, with those held back and tested, the sick, and those too weak to go.[[h_146_238_early_migrants:p1 | al-Waqidi]] And the Prophet ﷺ stayed on, waiting for Allah to give him permission to follow.[[h_9783_994_early_migrants:p1 | "the Messenger of Allah ﷺ stayed in Makkah after his companions, waiting to be permitted to emigrate"]]

## In depth

### Who was first?

The reports answer different questions. Ibn Ishaq names Abu Salama as the first of the Prophet's ﷺ companions from Quraysh to emigrate to Madinah, and dates it a year before the pledge at al-‘Aqabah; Ibn Sa‘d also names him as the first to arrive.[[h_23833_491_prep_780:p1, h_930_737_prep_abu_salama_date:p1 | Ibn Ishaq]][[h_146_237_permission:p1–p2 | al-Waqidi, from al-Zuhri and from ‘Urwah from ‘Aishah]] Al-Bara ibn ‘Azib, a man of Madinah, says "the first to come to us" were Mus‘ab ibn ‘Umayr and Ibn Umm Maktum: he is speaking of whom his own people saw arrive, and of two men who taught people the Qur'an there.[[h_930_743_prep_baraa_2:p1, h_930_744_prep_baraa_2:p1, h_1681_5917_hs_early_short_1:p1–p2 | al-Bara ibn ‘Azib, through al-Bukhari]] As for the first woman to reach Madinah in a camel litter, Ibn Sa‘d names Layla bint Abi Hathmah, the wife of ‘Amir ibn Rabi‘ah, who came right after Abu Salama. Musa ibn ‘Uqba, from al-Zuhri, says it is said to be Umm Salama, while some say Umm ‘Abdullah bint Abi Hathmah, "and Allah knows best".[[h_146_237_permission:p1–p2 | al-Waqidi, from al-Zuhri and from ‘Urwah from ‘Aishah]][[h_7478_980_ra_musa_early:p1–p2, h_7478_981_ra_musa_early:p1 | Musa ibn ‘Uqba from al-Zuhri]][[h_23833_493_prep_781:p1 | Ibn Ishaq]] Umm Salama's own account shows that she did not travel with her husband when he first left.[[h_23833_492_prep_cont:p1–p2 | Umm Salama, through Ibn Ishaq from his father from Salamah ibn ‘Abdullah]]

### When did they go?

In Ibn Ishaq's account Abu Salama left early, harmed by Quraysh after his return from Abyssinia, a year before the pledge. Ibn Sa‘d and Musa ibn ‘Uqba tell of the general departure: when Quraysh pressed the believers hard, the Prophet ﷺ told them to go, and they left in successive groups.[[h_23833_491_prep_780:p1, h_930_737_prep_abu_salama_date:p1 | Ibn Ishaq]][[h_146_237_permission:p1–p2 | al-Waqidi, from al-Zuhri and from ‘Urwah from ‘Aishah]][[h_7478_980_ra_musa_early:p1–p2, h_7478_981_ra_musa_early:p1 | Musa ibn ‘Uqba from al-Zuhri]] Ibn Sa‘d's report comes through his teacher al-Waqidi, who combines what al-Zuhri and ‘Urwah related from ‘Aishah. Musa ibn ‘Uqba adds that some companions, among them Sa‘d ibn Abi Waqqas, stayed in Makkah until after the Prophet ﷺ reached Madinah; Ibn Kathir points out that al-Bara's report in Muslim says plainly that Sa‘d came before, and calls that the correct view.[[h_7478_982_ra_musa_early:p1 | Musa ibn ‘Uqba from al-Zuhri]][[h_930_744_prep_saad_date:p1]]

### What became of ‘Ayyash and Hisham?

The verses ‘Umar sent are al-Zumar 39:53–55; in ‘Umar's report Hisham reads them, understands, and rides to Madinah.[[h_23833_498_prep_787:p1–p3, h_23833_499_prep_cont:p1–p4, h_7478_983_ra_umar_hisham:p1–p4 | ‘Umar, then Hisham ibn al-‘As]] Ibn Hisham, from someone he trusted, adds that al-Walid ibn al-Walid later went secretly to Makkah, cut the two men's fetters and brought them to Madinah.[[h_23833_499_prep_788:p1–p4 | Ibn Hisham, from "someone I trust"]] In Musa ibn ‘Uqba's account, ‘Ayyash stayed bound until he left with those who went before the conquest of Makkah, and the Prophet ﷺ used to pray for his release.[[h_7478_980_ra_musa_early:p1–p2, h_7478_981_ra_musa_early:p1 | Musa ibn ‘Uqba from al-Zuhri]]

### Two accounts of Suhayb

The story told above comes from Abu ‘Uthman al-Nahdi, who heard it second-hand: Ibn Hisham says only that it "was mentioned" to him, and Abu ‘Uthman that it "reached" him.[[h_23833_500_prep_791:p2–p3 | Ibn Hisham, "it was mentioned to me", from Abu ‘Uthman al-Nahdi, "it reached me"]] Suhayb's own account, through Sa‘id ibn al-Musayyab, recorded by al-Bayhaqi and quoted by Ibn Kathir, is set later. He meant to leave with the Prophet ﷺ and Abu Bakr, but young men of Quraysh stopped him; he slipped away, was caught, and bought his freedom with measures of gold buried under his doorstep, then reached the Prophet ﷺ at Quba. There the Prophet's ﷺ words were "Abu Yahya, the bargain has profited."[[h_930_745_prep_suhayb:p1–p2, h_930_746_prep_suhayb:p1 | Suhayb, through al-Bayhaqi]]

### Where they stayed, and who led the prayer

Ibn Ishaq records where the emigrants lodged. Abu Salama, ‘Amir ibn Rabi‘ah and the sons of Jahsh stayed with Mubashshir ibn ‘Abd al-Mundhir at Quba, among Banu ‘Amr ibn ‘Awf.[[h_23833_495_prep_cont:p1 | Ibn Ishaq]] ‘Abd al-Rahman ibn ‘Awf stayed with Sa‘d ibn al-Rabi‘, Mus‘ab ibn ‘Umayr with Sa‘d ibn Mu‘adh, and ‘Uthman ibn ‘Affan with Aws ibn Thabit, the brother of Hassan ibn Thabit. That, Ibn Ishaq says, is why Hassan loved ‘Uthman and wept for him when he was killed.[[h_23833_502_prep_794:p1, h_23833_502_prep_796:p1, h_23833_502_prep_798:p1 | Ibn Ishaq]]

Ibn Sa‘d says Salim led the emigrants in prayer at Quba. Ibn ‘Umar, in reports recorded by al-Bayhaqi and Ya‘qub ibn Sufyan, speaks of a smaller group: ‘Umar, Abu ‘Ubaydah and Salim stopped at a place he calls al-‘Usbah, and Salim led them because he knew the most Qur'an.[[h_146_238_early_migrants:p1, h_7478_983_ra_salim:p1, h_7478_984_ra_salim:p1–p2, h_930_747_prep_salim:p1–p2 | Salim at Quba; Ibn ‘Umar; Ibn ‘Umar, through Ya‘qub ibn Sufyan]]

### "This man's nephew"

Abu Jahl's words at the empty house were spoken with al-‘Abbas beside him, and the Prophet ﷺ was al-‘Abbas's nephew. Ibn Ishaq gives the words without naming whom "this man" meant.[[h_23833_493_prep_781:p1, h_23833_494_prep_cont:p1–p7 | Ibn Ishaq: Banu Jahsh, Abu Ahmad, the empty house, ‘Utbah and Abu Jahl]]

## For the reviewer

- **Decisions to confirm.** (1) The chapter opens on Abu Salama, whom Ibn Ishaq places a year before the pledge, then moves to the general permission after the seventy; In depth ("When did they go?") explains the two timelines. (2) The pledges at al-‘Aqabah get one sentence ("seventy men of Madinah pledged themselves…"), from Ibn Sa‘d; the al-‘Aqabah chapter tells them. (3) The permission is told through "it is Yathrib" and "brothers and a home". The Prophet's ﷺ earlier words about the land he had been shown ("a salt plain with palm trees, between two lava fields") are left to the preparations chapter, which opens with them from ‘Aishah's report. (4) Abu Jahl's "this man's nephew" is quoted as Ibn Ishaq gives it, and In depth notes that al-‘Abbas, the Prophet's ﷺ uncle, was there. (5) Suhayb is told from Abu ‘Uthman's indirect report, with one line of attribution. His own fuller account sets his escape after the Prophet ﷺ had left, so it is kept in In depth (it belongs in time with Quba). (6) ‘Ayyash's daylight parade rests on an unnamed family member and is marked "one of ‘Ayyash's family remembered". (7) The account is 965 words, a little over the 900 guide. Cutting further would mean losing Umm Salama's ride or ‘Umar's warning to ‘Ayyash, the two scenes that carry the chapter. Salim's prayer leadership and the list of hosts were moved to In depth to keep it near the guide. **Details pass:** the account now also carries ‘Ayyash's own reply, Hisham and the verses at Dhu Tuwa, Umm Salama's "no household in Islam", ‘Uthman ibn Talhah not yet being Muslim, and Salim leading the prayer, which brings it to about 1,160 words.
- **New readings** (all from the Core packets in `core/core.ar.json`, batch `preparations/earlier_emigrants`): Abu Salama as the Prophet's ﷺ cousin (note 1); Umm Salama's verdict on ‘Uthman ibn Talhah (5); al-Tabari's "brothers and a home", and the Prophet ﷺ waiting for permission (7, 20); the empty house of Banu Jahsh (8); the Ansar drawing lots (15); where the emigrants lodged (16); Sa‘d ibn Abi Waqqas's timing (23–24); Hisham and al-Walid (25–26); the editors' note on ‘Uthman ibn Talhah (28).
- **Gaps.** No passage gives a date or season for the general departure beyond "after the seventy" and "a year before the pledge" for Abu Salama, and none describes the road to Madinah apart from al-Tan‘im and the meeting place at al-Tanadub. Nothing explains al-Abtah, so it is left as a name.
- **Removed from the account:** the long lists of names and the hosts' genealogies (where they lodged is now in In depth), Abu Ahmad's verses and ‘Utbah's line of poetry, the rendezvous's landmarks (the pool of Banu Ghifar above Sarif), al-Bara's list of arrivals (In depth keeps "the first to come to us"), chains of narrators, the discussion of who counts as "first" (now in In depth), al-Walid's rescue of ‘Ayyash and Hisham and Musa ibn ‘Uqba's version of ‘Ayyash's fate, and ‘Uthman ibn Maz‘un's death in Umm al-‘Ala's house, which comes later.
- **Hand-overs.** The account opens on the first emigrant leaving Makkah, so the book starts inside the story. It ends with the Prophet ﷺ, Abu Bakr and ‘Ali left in Makkah and the Prophet ﷺ waiting for permission. That is where the departure chapter's opening ("While the Prophet ﷺ waited in Makkah for permission…") and the preparations chapter's waiting pick up. Al-‘Aqabah, next, goes back in time to tell how Madinah came to offer that home.
