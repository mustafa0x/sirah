# Suraqah's Pursuit

chapter: hch_suraqa
stage: pursuit
prefix: sp_suraqa_

**Question:** How did the horseman who rode out to capture the Prophet ﷺ end up turning his pursuers away?

## Overview

Quraysh had not let the Prophet ﷺ go. Their messengers went out to the tribes with an offer: the blood-money of each man, for the Prophet ﷺ and for Abu Bakr, to whoever killed or captured him.[[bukhari:p1 | Suraqah]] Among Banu Mudlij, Suraqah ibn Malik heard it, and he hoped to win it.[[ibn_ishaq_start:p1–p2 | Suraqah through Ibn Ishaq]] So when a man of his tribe walked into their gathering saying he had seen riders by the coast, Suraqah knew at once who they were, and told no one.[[bukhari:p1 | Suraqah]] What happened when he caught up with them, he told afterwards in his own words.[[bukhari:p1 | Suraqah]]

## A price on their heads

Suraqah remembered how the news first came: "Messengers of the unbelievers of Quraysh came to us," offering, for the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and for Abu Bakr, the blood-money of each to whoever killed or captured him.[[bukhari:p1 | Suraqah]]

One day Suraqah was sitting among his people, Banu Mudlij, when one of them came up and stood over them. "Suraqah," he said, "just now I saw dark figures by the coast. I think they are Muhammad and his companions."[[bukhari:p1 | Suraqah]]

"I knew that it was them," Suraqah said. But aloud he told the man, "It is not them. You saw so-and-so and so-and-so, who set out before our eyes."[[bukhari:p1 | Suraqah]] In Ibn Ishaq's telling he silenced the man with a look first, then said they were only a family out searching for a lost animal. "Perhaps," the man said, and fell silent. Suraqah had his own plan: "I hoped to bring him back to Quraysh and take the hundred she-camels."[[ibn_ishaq_start:p1–p2 | Suraqah through Ibn Ishaq]]

## Out by the back of the house

He sat on in the gathering for a while, then rose and went inside. He told his servant girl to take his horse out and hold it for him behind a hillock. He took his spear and left by the back of the house, its butt dragging along the ground and its tip held low, until he reached his horse, mounted, and urged it on.[[bukhari:p1 | Suraqah]]

He rode hard, and as he drew near them, his horse stumbled and he fell. He got up, reached into his quiver and drew out his divining arrows, the arrows he used to draw lots, and put the question to them: should I harm them or not? Out came the answer he hated. He mounted again, defying the arrows, and rode on until he could hear the Messenger of Allah ﷺ reciting. The Prophet ﷺ did not look back. Abu Bakr kept looking back.[[bukhari:p2 | Suraqah]]

## Allah is with us

Abu Bakr remembered the same moments from in front. They had set out after midday, with people searching for them, and none caught up but Suraqah ibn Malik on his horse.[[abu_bakr:p4–p5 | Abu Bakr through al-Bara (route of Zuhayr); the Prophet ﷺ]][[H-7478-1005-RA-BARA-SURAQA | Abu Bakr through al-Bara (route of Isra'il), quoting the Prophet ﷺ and Suraqah]] They were on hard ground. "Messenger of Allah, we have been overtaken!" Abu Bakr said. "Do not grieve," the Prophet ﷺ answered. "Allah is with us."[[abu_bakr:p4–p5 | Abu Bakr through al-Bara (route of Zuhayr); the Prophet ﷺ]]

When the horseman was only two or three spear-lengths away, Abu Bakr said it again, and wept. "Why are you weeping?" the Prophet ﷺ asked. "By Allah, it is not for myself that I weep," Abu Bakr said, "but for you." The Prophet ﷺ prayed: "O Allah, suffice us against him however You will."[[H-7478-1005-RA-BARA-SURAQA | Abu Bakr through al-Bara (route of Isra'il), quoting the Prophet ﷺ and Suraqah]]

Then the ground gave way. "My horse's forelegs sank into the earth up to the knees," Suraqah said, "and I fell off it." He shouted at the horse and it rose, barely able to pull its forelegs free, and when it stood upright, dust rose from the marks of its legs into the sky like smoke.[[bukhari:p3–p5 | Suraqah, quoting the Prophet ﷺ]] Abu Bakr remembered the horse sinking to its belly, and Suraqah leaping clear of it. "Muhammad, I know this is your doing," Suraqah said. "Pray to Allah to save me from what I am in, and by Allah, I will blind the pursuers behind me." He meant that he would hide them from those still searching.[[H-7478-1005-RA-BARA-SURAQA | Abu Bakr through al-Bara (route of Isra'il), quoting the Prophet ﷺ and Suraqah]][[lexicon:p2]]

## "Keep our whereabouts hidden"

Once more Suraqah cast his arrows, and once more the answer was the one he hated. This time he called out to them, asking for safety, and they stopped.[[bukhari:p3–p5 | Suraqah, quoting the Prophet ﷺ]] "I am Suraqah ibn Ju‘shum," he called, in Ibn Ishaq's version. "Wait for me so I can speak to you. By Allah, I will give you no cause for alarm, and nothing you dislike will come to you from me."[[ibn_ishaq_start:p2, ibn_ishaq_reply:p1–p4, ibn_ishaq_end:p1 | Suraqah through Ibn Ishaq]]

He rode up to them. "When I met with what I met, being held back from them," he said, "it came into my mind that the cause of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ would prevail."[[bukhari:p3–p5 | Suraqah, quoting the Prophet ﷺ]]

He told them about the price on their heads and what people meant to do to them, and offered them provisions and goods. "They took nothing from me and asked nothing of me," he said, "except that he said: 'Keep our whereabouts hidden.'"[[bukhari:p3–p5 | Suraqah, quoting the Prophet ﷺ]] Abu Bakr recalled that he offered more: "Here is my quiver; take an arrow from it. You will pass my camels and sheep at such-and-such a place; take what you need from them." "We have no need of your camels and sheep," the Prophet ﷺ said, and he prayed for him.[[H-7478-1005-RA-BARA-SURAQA | Abu Bakr through al-Bara (route of Isra'il), quoting the Prophet ﷺ and Suraqah]]

Then Suraqah asked for a letter of safety, and at the Prophet's ﷺ word ‘Amir ibn Fuhayrah wrote it on a piece of leather.[[bukhari:p3–p5 | Suraqah, quoting the Prophet ﷺ]] In Ibn Ishaq's version the Prophet ﷺ told Abu Bakr, "Ask him what he wants from us," and Suraqah asked for "a letter that will be a sign between you and me". "Write it for him, Abu Bakr," the Prophet ﷺ said. Abu Bakr wrote it on a bone, a scrap or a potsherd, and tossed it to him; Suraqah put it in his quiver.[[ibn_ishaq_start:p2, ibn_ishaq_reply:p1–p4, ibn_ishaq_end:p1 | Suraqah through Ibn Ishaq]] Then the Messenger of Allah ﷺ went on his way.[[bukhari:p3–p5 | Suraqah, quoting the Prophet ﷺ]]

## The hunter turns them back

Suraqah turned back. "Allah is my witness," he had promised them, "that I will turn the pursuers back from you."[[abu_bakr:p6–p10 | Abu Bakr through al-Bara (route of Zuhayr), quoting Suraqah]] To everyone he met on the way, he said: "You have been spared the search here." "He did not meet anyone without turning him back," Abu Bakr said, "and he kept his word to us."[[abu_bakr:p6–p10 | Abu Bakr through al-Bara (route of Zuhayr), quoting Suraqah]] He told the searchers: "Go back. I have searched this ground for you, and you know my eye for tracks."[[H-146-244-MABAD-AND-SURAQA | ‘Abd al-Malik, "it reached us"]]

Of what had passed between them, Suraqah said nothing to anyone.[[ibn_ishaq_start:p2, ibn_ishaq_reply:p1–p4, ibn_ishaq_end:p1 | Suraqah through Ibn Ishaq]] But he kept the letter. Years later, after the conquest of Makkah, Hunayn and al-Ta'if, he went with it to meet the Prophet ﷺ at al-Ji‘ranah. Horsemen of the Ansar struck at him with their spears, crying, "Back! Back!", until he came near, raised his hand with the letter and said, "Messenger of Allah, this is your letter to me." "A day of faithfulness and kindness," the Prophet ﷺ said. "Come near." There Suraqah accepted Islam.[[ibn_ishaq_end:p2–p4 | Suraqah through Ibn Ishaq, quoting the Prophet ﷺ]]

That day, the Prophet ﷺ and Abu Bakr rode on towards Madinah,[[H-7478-1005-RA-BARA-SURAQA | Abu Bakr through al-Bara (route of Isra'il), quoting the Prophet ﷺ and Suraqah]] with the hunter behind them sending every pursuer home.[[abu_bakr:p6–p10 | Abu Bakr through al-Bara (route of Zuhayr), quoting Suraqah]]

## In depth

### Two tellings of Suraqah's story

Ibn Ishaq's telling, like al-Bukhari's, goes back to Suraqah himself, but the two differ in their details.[[bukhari:p1 | Suraqah]][[ibn_ishaq_start:p1–p2 | Suraqah through Ibn Ishaq]] In al-Bukhari, Quraysh offer the blood-money of each man for killing or capturing him; Suraqah's horse stumbles once, he casts the arrows, rides on, and then its forelegs sink; ‘Amir ibn Fuhayrah writes the letter on leather.[[bukhari:p1 | Suraqah]][[bukhari:p2 | Suraqah]][[bukhari:p3–p5 | Suraqah, quoting the Prophet ﷺ]] In Ibn Ishaq, and in the same story as Musa ibn ‘Uqbah heard it from al-Zuhri, the price is a hundred she-camels for bringing the Prophet ﷺ back; Suraqah puts on his armour and casts the arrows before he sets out ("He will not harm him"); the horse stumbles twice and he falls each time, asking "What is this?"; at the third fall its forelegs go into the ground; and Abu Bakr writes the letter, on a bone, a scrap or a potsherd.[[ibn_ishaq_start:p1–p2 | Suraqah through Ibn Ishaq]][[ibn_ishaq_start:p2, ibn_ishaq_reply:p1–p4, ibn_ishaq_end:p1 | Suraqah through Ibn Ishaq]][[H-7478-1008-RA-MUSA-SURAQA, H-7478-1009-RA-MUSA-SURAQA | Suraqah through Musa ibn ‘Uqbah from al-Zuhri]] The story above follows al-Bukhari and names Ibn Ishaq where his version adds or differs.

Who wrote the letter is a known difference. Al-Maqrizi gives both names, Abu Bakr, "and it is said ‘Amir ibn Fuhayrah".[[H-1524-99-CF4-AMAN-WRITERS]] A note on al-Qadi ‘Iyad's *al-Shifa* reports a way of reconciling them: ‘Amir wrote first, and Abu Bakr afterwards.[[H-1753-358-CP2-AMAN-WRITERS | "it is said"]]

### Abu Bakr's side of the story

Abu Bakr told his part to ‘Azib, al-Bara's father, who would not let him take home a saddle he had bought until he told how he and the Prophet ﷺ had fared on the road with the idolaters searching for them.[[H-7478-1004-RA-BARA-SURAQA, H-7478-1005-RA-BARA-OTHER | al-Bara (route of Isra'il); al-Bayhaqi on al-Bukhari and Muslim]] Al-Bara heard it, and al-Bayhaqi notes that al-Bukhari and Muslim both recorded it.[[H-7478-1004-RA-BARA-SURAQA, H-7478-1005-RA-BARA-OTHER | al-Bara (route of Isra'il); al-Bayhaqi on al-Bukhari and Muslim]][[abu_bakr:p1–p3, abu_bakr:p11 | al-Bayhaqi on Muslim and al-Bukhari (route of Zuhayr)]] The two men saw the same moments from opposite ends: Suraqah tells of his arrows and his own change of heart; Abu Bakr of his fear, the Prophet's ﷺ prayer, and Suraqah's plea for a prayer in return. Suraqah says the forelegs sank to the knees; Abu Bakr says the horse sank to its belly.[[H-7478-1005-RA-BARA-SURAQA | Abu Bakr through al-Bara (route of Isra'il), quoting the Prophet ﷺ and Suraqah]][[bukhari:p3–p5 | Suraqah, quoting the Prophet ﷺ]][[abu_bakr:p6–p10 | Abu Bakr through al-Bara (route of Zuhayr), quoting Suraqah]]

### Words in the story

*Azlam* (in Ibn Ishaq, *qidah*) are the arrows Suraqah cast to draw lots, asking whether to harm the travellers or not; his *kinanah* is the quiver he kept them in.[[bukhari:p2 | Suraqah]][[ibn_ishaq_start:p1–p2 | Suraqah through Ibn Ishaq]] The *tullab* are the pursuers: the word, says Isma‘il al-Isbahani, is used for one pursuer or for many. Suraqah's promise *la-u‘ammiyanna*, "I will blind them", means "I will hide you from them".[[lexicon:p2]]

### Where and when

Ibn Sa‘d records a report, introduced only with "it reached us", that the Prophet ﷺ left the cave on Monday night, when four nights of Rabi‘ al-Awwal had passed, rested at midday on Tuesday at Qudayd, and was met by Suraqah after setting out from there. In this report Suraqah tells the searchers: "Go back. I have searched this ground for you, and you know my eye for tracks."[[H-146-244-MABAD-AND-SURAQA | ‘Abd al-Malik, "it reached us"]]

Scholars differed over whether Suraqah came before or after Umm Ma‘bad's tent. Al-Salihi, following Mughaltay, put Umm Ma‘bad first and called that the correct order; Ibn Hibban, al-Bayhaqi, Ibn ‘Abd al-Barr, Ibn al-Jawzi and Ibn Kathir told Suraqah first, the order this journey follows.[[H-122236-155-CP2-MABAD-ORDER-A, H-122236-156-CP2-MABAD-ORDER-B | editor's note quoting al-Salihi]]

### The letter at al-Ji‘ranah

Suraqah kept the letter. In Ibn Ishaq's account he brought it to the Prophet ﷺ at al-Ji‘ranah, after the conquest of Makkah, Hunayn and al-Ta'if, held it up and said, "Messenger of Allah, this is your letter to me." "A day of faithfulness and kindness," the Prophet ﷺ said. "Come near." There Suraqah accepted Islam.[[ibn_ishaq_end:p2–p4 | Suraqah through Ibn Ishaq, quoting the Prophet ﷺ]] The well-known saying that Suraqah would one day wear the bracelets of Kisra belongs to that later life, not to the Hijrah; al-‘Azimi counts it among the weak reports, since its chain is broken.[[H-38114-662-CP1-BRACELETS-A, H-38114-663-CP1-BRACELETS-B]]

## For the reviewer

- **Decisions to confirm.** (1) Al-Bukhari's first-person account of Suraqah is the spine; Ibn Ishaq is named three times in the account, only where his version adds a quotation or differs (the hundred she-camels, the look and the lost animal, Suraqah's call, the writer and material of the letter). (2) Abu Bakr's account is woven in as the view from in front ("Allah is with us", the weeping, the prayer, the horse to its belly), with Suraqah's knees and Abu Bakr's belly both told plainly rather than reconciled. (3) Ibn Ishaq's pre-departure lot and the double stumble are left to In depth to keep one clear line of action. (4) "He kept silent and told no one" (Ibn Ishaq) sits beside "he turned back everyone he met" (Abu Bakr): I read the silence as about the meeting, the turning-back as about the search; confirm this pairing reads as intended. (5) The title is changed to "Suraqah's Pursuit" (the spec's example); the old title "Suraqa: From Pursuit to Safety" is misspelt and gives away the ending.
- **New readings:** Abu Bakr's fuller account through Isra'il (notes 5, 12), which supplies the weeping, the prayer's wording, Suraqah's "this is your doing", the arrow and herds, and "Madinah" as the destination; Musa ibn ‘Uqbah's telling (9); the writers of the letter (10–11); Ibn Sa‘d's Qudayd report (15); the order of Suraqah and Umm Ma‘bad (16); al-‘Azimi on the bracelets (18).
- **Gaps:** none the story needs. Where Banu Mudlij lived and the exact spot of the meeting are not given by these readings; Ibn Sa‘d's Qudayd rests on an unattributed report and stays in In depth. The scene before the pursuit in Abu Bakr's Isra'il account (resting in a rock's shade, the shepherd's milk) is not told here; it belongs to the road chapter if it is wanted there.
- **Hand-overs.** Opens with Quraysh's offer and the "dark figures by the coast", picking up the coastal road the road chapter ends on; does not retell the guide or ‘Amir's joining. Ends with the Prophet ﷺ riding on towards Madinah and the pursuit called off, ready for the next stop at Umm Ma‘bad's tent.
- **Removed:** the narrator chain to Suraqah, the "tour" of two perspectives in the old In depth, the glossary of *adim* and the forelegs, and the later Ji‘ranah meeting from the account (kept in In depth).
