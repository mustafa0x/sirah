# Building the Prophet's Mosque

chapter: hch_mosque
stage: arrival
prefix: sp_mosque_chapter_

**Question:** How did a yard for drying dates become the Prophet's ﷺ mosque, and who built it?

## Overview

The camel had knelt in a yard where dates were spread out to dry, in the quarter of Banu al-Najjar.[[ishaq_site:p1, context_urwa_end:p2, H-1673-4074-HS-MIRBAD-OWNERSHIP-1]] Two orphan boys owned it,[[ishaq_site:p1]] and some of the Muslims already prayed there in the open.[[context_urwa_end:p2]] The Prophet ﷺ had no mosque in Madinah yet; he prayed wherever the time of prayer found him.[[muslim_site:p2, marabid:p2]] Now he wanted this ground for one.[[ishaq_work:p1, muslim_site:p2]] But the yard was crowded with palms, old graves and ruins,[[muslim_site:p5]] and the people he offered to pay had other ideas about its price.[[muslim_site:p3–p4, context_anas_parallel:p2–p3]][[context_urwa_end:p4–p5]] Everything would have to be cleared, and built by hand.[[context_urwa_end:p6, stages:p1]]

## A yard for drying dates

The place where the camel had knelt, in the quarter of Banu Malik ibn al-Najjar, was a mirbad: an open yard where dates were laid out to dry.[[ishaq_site:p1, context_urwa_end:p2, H-1673-4074-HS-MIRBAD-OWNERSHIP-1]] It belonged to two orphan boys of the clan, Sahl and Suhayl, the sons of ‘Amr.[[ishaq_site:p1]] Some of the Muslim men of Madinah were already praying there.[[context_urwa_end:p2]]

The Prophet ﷺ had no mosque yet. Until now he had prayed wherever the time of prayer found him, even in the pens where sheep were kept.[[muslim_site:p2, marabid:p2]] Abu Ayyub had carried his baggage into his house, and the Prophet ﷺ asked about the yard: whose was it?[[ishaq_lodging:p2–p3]] Mu‘adh ibn ‘Afra answered: "Messenger of Allah, it belongs to Sahl and Suhayl, the sons of ‘Amr. They are orphans in my care. I will satisfy them for it, so take it for a mosque."[[ishaq_lodging:p4]]

## A price no one would take

The Prophet ﷺ ordered that a mosque be built there, and sent for the leading men of Banu al-Najjar.[[ishaq_work:p1, muslim_site:p2]] When they came, he said: "Banu al-Najjar, name me a price for this walled plot of yours." "No, by Allah," they said. "We seek its price only from Allah."[[muslim_site:p3–p4, context_anas_parallel:p2–p3]]

He called the two boys as well, and bargained with them for the yard, so that he could make it a mosque. "No," they said. "We give it to you, Messenger of Allah."[[context_urwa_end:p4–p5]] Anas, who tells of the clan's answer, says nothing of money changing hands. Other reports remember that the Prophet ﷺ would not take the yard as a gift, and bought it from the two boys.[[H-9898-138-prep-1, H-1673-4074-HS-MIRBAD-OWNERSHIP-1, context_musa_start:p6]] The historian al-Waqidi gives the price as ten dinars, and says Abu Bakr paid it for him.[[price_foot:p3, H-146-250-MOSQUE-A, H-1673-4094-HS-MIRBAD-1]]

## Clearing the ground

The plot was far from empty. Anas listed what was in it: date palms, the graves of polytheists, and ruins. The Prophet ﷺ ordered the palms cut down, the graves dug up and the ruins levelled.[[muslim_site:p5]] The felled palms were set in a row along the side that faced the qibla, the direction of prayer, and the posts on either side of the doorway were made of stone.[[muslim_work:p1, H-1673-4094-HS-GRAVES-1]] The walls would be raised from labin: bricks of clay, dried but not fired.[[context_urwa_end:p6, stages:p1]]

## Carrying the bricks

The Prophet ﷺ did not stand and watch. He carried bricks alongside the others.[[context_urwa_end:p6, stages:p1]] He worked on the mosque himself to make the Muslims eager to work on it, and the Emigrants from Makkah and the Ansar, the Helpers of Madinah, threw themselves into the work.[[ishaq_work:p1]] One of the Muslims put it in a line of verse: "If we sit idle while the Prophet works, that would be misguided work on our part."[[ishaq_work:p2–p3]] ‘Ali ibn Abi Talib had lines of his own: "Not equal are the one who builds mosques, toiling in them standing and sitting, and the one who keeps away from the dust."[[context_musa_start:p7–p8, context_musa_end:p1–p3, critique:p1–p5, builders:p3–p4]]

As they hauled the stone, they chanted rajaz, short rhythmic verses, and the Prophet ﷺ was among them: "O Allah, there is no good but the good of the Hereafter, so help the Ansar and the Emigrants."[[muslim_work:p1–p2, context_anas_parallel:p5]] And the Prophet ﷺ would say: "There is no life but the life of the Hereafter. O Allah, have mercy on the Emigrants and the Ansar."[[ishaq_work:p4–p8]] As he carried the bricks, he would also say: "This load is not the load of Khaybar; this, our Lord, is more righteous and purer."[[context_musa_start:p7–p8, context_musa_end:p1–p3, critique:p1–p5, builders:p3–p4]]

Abu Sa‘id al-Khudri remembered the work years later. "We carried the bricks one at a time," he said, "and ‘Ammar two at a time." The Prophet ﷺ saw ‘Ammar and began brushing the dust from him.[[H-7478-1067-RA-AMMAR-KHALID-A]] He asked him, "‘Ammar, won't you carry as your companions carry?" "I want the reward from Allah," ‘Ammar replied.[[H-7478-1068-RA-AMMAR-KHALID-B]] As he brushed the dust away, he said: "Woe to ‘Ammar, the rebel party will kill him. He calls them to Paradise, and they call him to the Fire." And ‘Ammar said, "I seek refuge in Allah from trials."[[H-1681-4456-AMMAR-2812, H-1681-753-AMMAR-447]][[H-7478-1067-RA-AMMAR-KHALID-A, H-7478-1069-RA-AMMAR-CRITIC-A, H-7478-1070-RA-AMMAR-CRITIC-B]] Long afterwards, when ‘Ammar was killed at Siffin, ‘Abdullah ibn ‘Amr reminded his father of those words, and of the day the Messenger of Allah ﷺ built the mosque.[[H-7478-1072-RA-AMMAR-SIFFIN-MEMORY, H-7478-1073-RA-AMMAR-SIFFIN-MEMORY]]

## A roof of palm fronds

The mosque that rose from the yard was simple. In the Prophet's ﷺ lifetime, ‘Abdullah ibn ‘Umar said, it was built of mud brick, roofed with palm fronds, and held up by pillars of palm trunks.[[H-7478-1061-RA-MOSQUE-IBNUMAR, H-7478-1062-RA-MOSQUE-IBNUMAR]] Its prayer wall faced Jerusalem: for their first seventeen months in Madinah, Mu‘adh ibn Jabal recalled, the Prophet ﷺ prayed towards it.[[H-9862-287-ISHAQ-EARLY-ADHAN-01, H-146-251-MOSQUE-B]] When the Ansar gathered money and asked him to build it up and adorn it ("How long are we to pray under these palm fronds?"), he answered: "I have no wish to turn away from my brother Moses. A shelter like the shelter of Moses."[[H-7478-1063-RA-MOSQUE-UBADA]]

Long afterwards, ‘Umar ibn al-Khattab stood before the people and reminded them how it had begun: "This mosque was built by the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, and we were with him, the Emigrants and the Ansar. So pray in it, and whoever of you cannot find a place, let him prostrate on his brother's back."[[H-7478-1066-RA-MOSQUE-UMAR]]

Beside the mosque rose rooms for the Prophet ﷺ himself; until both were finished, he stayed on in Abu Ayyub's house.[[ishaq_work:p1]] And while the work was still going on, the families left behind in Makkah began to arrive.[[H-1673-4077-HS-FAMILY-1]]

## In depth

### Whose yard was it, and was it bought?

The reports name the boys' guardian differently. In Ibn Ishaq's account it is Mu‘adh ibn ‘Afra; in ‘Urwah's report in al-Bukhari, and in al-Zuhri's in al-Bayhaqi, it is As‘ad ibn Zurarah, one of the first of the Ansar to accept Islam. Al-Zubayr ibn Bakkar names Abu Ayyub. Ibn Hajar judges As‘ad the better-established name, and suggests the boys may have been in more than one man's care, or passed from one guardian to the next after As‘ad.[[context_urwa_end:p2, H-1673-4074-HS-MIRBAD-OWNERSHIP-1, agency:p2–p3]]

They differ on the price, too. In Anas's report the leading men of Banu al-Najjar refuse any price, and Ibn Hajar notes that the plain sense of his words is that no price was taken; the biographers, he says, report otherwise.[[muslim_site:p4, H-1673-1012-HS-PRICE-1]] In ‘Urwah's report the boys offer the yard as a gift. Al-Zuhri gives three versions without choosing between them: the boys gave it; As‘ad offered them palms of his own in the quarter of Banu Bayadah in exchange; or the Prophet ﷺ bought it from them.[[context_musa_start:p3–p6]] Ibn Hajar cites Musa ibn ‘Uqba, from al-Zuhri, for the ten dinars, and al-Waqidi for Abu Bakr paying them.[[price_foot:p3, H-146-250-MOSQUE-A, H-1673-4094-HS-MIRBAD-1]] He reconciles the reports this way: when the clan said they sought its price only from Allah, the Prophet ﷺ asked who owned it, they named the two boys, and he bought it from them; those who had refused payment may then have paid the boys on his behalf. Al-Zubayr says Abu Ayyub settled with them.[[context_urwa_end:p2, H-1673-4074-HS-MIRBAD-OWNERSHIP-1, agency:p2–p3]]

### A yard, a garden, or both?

Anas calls the plot a *ha'it*, a walled garden, while ‘Urwah calls it a *mirbad*, a drying yard. Ibn Hajar suggests it had once been a garden that fell to ruin and became a drying yard, which would explain the palms and the ruins in it, or that part was garden and part was yard. One of Anas's transmitters, Hammad ibn Salamah, has "tilled land" where others have "ruins".[[H-1673-4094-HS-MIRBAD-1, H-7478-1061-RA-MOSQUE-HAMMAD]]

### An open shelter first?

The stages of the building are reported differently. Ibn Hajar cites ‘Attaf ibn Khalid, in Ibn ‘A'idh, for the Prophet ﷺ praying there for twelve days while it was still an open shelter, before he built and roofed it; and al-Zubayr, from Anas, for a first building of palm fronds, rebuilt in mud brick four years after the Hijrah.[[stages:p2–p3]] Ibn ‘Umar's description above is of the mosque as it stood in the Prophet's ﷺ lifetime, before later caliphs enlarged it.[[H-7478-1061-RA-MOSQUE-IBNUMAR, H-7478-1062-RA-MOSQUE-IBNUMAR]]

### Whose words were the verses?

The reports agree that the builders chanted, and that the Prophet ﷺ joined in, but differ on the wording and on who said what. Anas gives the chant as the workers' own, "so help the Ansar and the Emigrants"; in Hammad's version it is "so forgive".[[muslim_work:p1–p2, context_anas_parallel:p5]][[H-1673-4094-HS-MIRBAD-1, H-7478-1061-RA-MOSQUE-HAMMAD]] Ibn Ishaq gives the Muslims a rhymed couplet, which Ibn Hisham remarks is "speech, not rajaz", and the Prophet ﷺ a plain version of it.[[ishaq_work:p4–p8]] Al-Zuhri gives the Prophet ﷺ two lines as he carried bricks, "This load is not the load of Khaybar; this, our Lord, is more righteous and purer" and "O Allah, there is no good but the good of the Hereafter, so have mercy on the Ansar and the Emigrants", and says these are the only lines of verse he heard of the Prophet ﷺ quoting, from a Muslim whose name he was not told.[[context_musa_start:p7–p8, context_musa_end:p1–p3, critique:p1–p5, builders:p3–p4]] Ibn Hajar answers the objection that the Prophet ﷺ did not recite poetry: what was denied him was composing it, not quoting it. Another route he cites, through Umm Salamah, adds lines by ‘Ali: the one who builds mosques, toiling in them standing and sitting, is not like the one who keeps away from the dust.[[context_musa_start:p7–p8, context_musa_end:p1–p3, critique:p1–p5, builders:p3–p4]]

### ‘Ammar and the words about his death

Al-Bukhari records these words in Abu Sa‘id's report of the building, through ‘Ikrimah: in his chapter on jihad (no. 2812) without any doubt, and in his chapter on mosques (no. 447) in some transmissions of the Sahih (Ibn al-Sakan's and Karimah's) but not in others (Abu Dharr's and al-Asili's).[[H-1681-4456-AMMAR-2812, H-1681-753-AMMAR-447]] That is why al-Bayhaqi, working from a copy without them, thought al-Bukhari had left the phrase out, perhaps because another student of Abu Sa‘id, Abu Nadrah, placed it at the digging of the Trench, years later; he allows that the Prophet ﷺ may have said it on both occasions.[[H-7478-1067-RA-AMMAR-KHALID-A, H-7478-1069-RA-AMMAR-CRITIC-A, H-7478-1070-RA-AMMAR-CRITIC-B]] At the battle of Siffin, ‘Abdullah ibn ‘Amr recalled those words, and the day the mosque was built, when ‘Ammar was killed there.[[H-7478-1072-RA-AMMAR-SIFFIN-MEMORY, H-7478-1073-RA-AMMAR-SIFFIN-MEMORY]]

## For the reviewer

- **Decisions to confirm.** (1) Mu‘adh ibn ‘Afra is quoted as the guardian, with "as Ibn Ishaq tells it"; As‘ad ibn Zurarah and the other names are in In depth. (2) The price is told as a plain difference in the account: Anas's clan refuses any price, other reports have the Prophet ﷺ insisting on buying; the ten dinars and Abu Bakr's payment are named as al-Waqidi's (a weaker report). Ibn Hajar's reconciliation stays in In depth. (3) "The rebel party will kill him" is told in the story: al-Bukhari records it in Abu Sa‘id's report of the building (no. 2812; no. 447 in some transmissions), owner-confirmed 2026-10-06; In depth explains al-Bayhaqi's remark and the Trench. (4) ‘Ammar's exchange ("I want the reward from Allah") rests on one route and is marked "in one telling". (5) ‘Ubadah's report of the Ansar asking to adorn the mosque has no date; it is placed after the building, as its wording ("How long are we to pray under these palm fronds?") implies the mosque was already in use. (6) ‘Umar's sermon is undated; "long afterwards" is used. (7) The previous chapter tells the camel kneeling and Abu Ayyub taking the baggage, so this chapter opens on the yard and refers to the baggage in one clause. The arrival of the families is only announced in the last sentence and left to `hch_settlement`.
- **New readings:** what a mirbad is, the guardians and the reconciled price (Ibn Hajar); the refused gift (Ibn Hibban); ten dinars and Abu Bakr (Ibn Sa‘d, Ibn Hajar); the doorposts; ‘Ammar's two bricks and al-Bayhaqi's notes on them; Siffin; Ibn ‘Umar's description; the qibla towards Jerusalem (Mu‘adh ibn Jabal, Ibn Sa‘d); the shelter of Moses (‘Ubadah); ‘Umar's sermon; Hammad's variant; families arriving during the building (notes 1, 10–11, 13, 19–24, 26, 29–30, 33–34).
- **Gaps:** the date the building began or ended, and how long it took, are not in the readings (only the twelve days of an open shelter, in ‘Attaf's report, and al-Zubayr's four years, both in In depth). The Khaybar line is not explained, because no reading says what "the load of Khaybar" means; it stays in In depth, quoted. The first prayer in the finished mosque is not described.
- **Left out:** al-Waqidi's dimensions, stone foundations, three doors and the draining of standing water (a weaker report with much detail; available if wanted); the reburial of bones from the graves (al-Waqidi); al-Hasan's report of the Prophet's ﷺ chest growing dusty (a Follower's report without a Companion); Talq ibn ‘Ali mixing clay, since he came with a later delegation; As‘ad ibn Zurarah's death before the mosque was finished (al-Tabari), which could open `hch_settlement`; the marriage to ‘Aishah and Sawdah's room (al-Waqidi), which belong after the building.
- **Removed from the account:** chains of narrators, the edition footnote's al-Nawawi note, Ibn Hisham's remark on rajaz, Ibn al-Tin's objections and Ibn Hajar's legal discussion of selling for orphans (now in In depth or left in the readings).
